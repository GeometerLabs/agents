# Pitch and sell the Stock orders template

For local Operators (18 and over) and builders. Written 17 November 2026, in the pilot phase. The shop owner never reads this guide: they get a short sheet from you. This is the English master; the Hindi, Malayalam and Kannada guides are translations of it.

**Read section 11 first.** Stock orders is not live in any shop yet. Today you can explain it, tell the five-step walkthrough in section 4 from your phone, and ask for a letter of intent by WhatsApp. You cannot promise a start date.

**Your part, in one line.** You are the person who looks after this service with its owner: you know the shop, you tune its limits, you fix things when they go wrong, and you are the person the owner calls. Setting it up is only the first step and is never the pitch; what lasts is care and judgment.

**What Stock orders does, in one breath.** The owner of a salon, a small shop or an input dealer writes on WhatsApp "order 10 boxes of hair colour from Ravi Traders". The helper works out the exact order from the owner's own supplier list and prices, shows it to the owner as a card, and only when the owner says yes does it send the order to the supplier, keep the month's budget for it, and ask again if the supplier goes quiet. When the goods come, the owner marks them received.

**The one claim you may make,** word for word: "No order goes to a supplier without your yes, and it is always the exact order you saw."

**What the owner does today.** Most owners phone or WhatsApp the supplier themselves and keep the quantities in their head or a notebook. It is free and familiar. What goes wrong is small and costly: the same order sent twice because the supplier did not answer, a wrong pack ordered, or a month's spending nobody added up.

## 1. Is this shop a fit?

| A good fit | Not a fit (for now) |
| --- | --- |
| Salons and parlours that reorder colour, shampoo and towels from a few known suppliers | A shop that buys from a different stranger every time |
| Small retailers and input dealers with a regular supplier list | A shop that wants the helper to pay the supplier: it does not pay anyone |
| An owner who wants a monthly limit on what is bought | A shop with no WhatsApp number, or suppliers who do not use WhatsApp |
| A shop where the same item comes by the box and by the piece | A buyer who wants quotes compared between suppliers (not built) |

### Ask yourself

1. Does the owner reorder from the same few suppliers, again and again?
2. Has an order ever been doubled, forgotten or the wrong pack?
3. Would the owner like a monthly limit that holds even on a busy day?

Three yes: go. Two yes: go, but carefully. One or none: skip it.

## 2. Before you go

1. Practise with a friend who plays the owner. Do sections 3 and 5 out loud, three times.
2. Tell the walkthrough (section 4) yourself, twice, start to finish: once in English and once in the owner's language.
3. Visit a friendly shop first, then a shop you do not know. A "no" is normal. Write down what they said.
4. Know your price before you walk in (section 6). Write it on a card.
5. Write to hello@geometer.dev to say hello and to ask for help.

### Help: write to hello@geometer.dev

Help is one message away. Write before your first visit and say hello.

## 3. The first three sentences

1. "When you run low on colour or shampoo, you write the order on WhatsApp, the way you always do."
2. "The helper turns it into the exact order, with the supplier, the pack, the price and the total, and shows it to you first."
3. "Nothing goes to the supplier until you say yes, and it keeps your month's limit for you."

Setup is the first step, not the pitch: the pitch is that someone looks after it with the owner.

## 4. Show it to the owner

Test money, nothing real sent. Use the example shop on your own phone, or tell it as a story:

1. The owner writes: "order 10 boxes of hair colour from Ravi Traders".
2. The helper shows a card: 10 × hair colour (box), ₹450 per box, total ₹4,500, from Ravi Traders, and what is left of the month's budget. The owner taps Approve.
3. The order goes to Ravi Traders as a WhatsApp message. Ravi taps OK.
4. The owner is told: "Ravi confirmed. This means they will supply it, not that it has arrived."
5. When the goods come, the owner writes "received po-1". If part came, "received po-1 6". If it is the wrong item, "wrong po-1".

Then show the two things owners remember: if the owner writes "10 hair colour" and it comes by the box and by the piece, the helper asks which one, and it never guesses. And if the supplier's reply is lost, the helper asks the supplier about the same order, and never sends a second one.

## 5. Questions owners ask

**"What if it makes a mistake?"** It cannot send an order you did not approve, and the card shows the exact supplier, item, pack, quantity, price and total. If anything changes after you saw the card, the old yes does not count and you are asked again.

**"Who sees my messages?"** The owner and the supplier see their own conversation. The shop's record keeps what was done; a supplier's words are kept only as a fingerprint.

**"Meta gives AI for free, why pay?"** Free AI answers messages. This keeps a monthly limit, never doubles an order when a supplier goes quiet, and keeps what the supplier said apart from what actually arrived. And someone looks after it with you.

**"Will it pay my supplier?"** No. You pay as you do today. You can write "paid po-1" as a note; the helper cannot check it.

**"What if the supplier does not answer?"** It asks again about the same order after four hours and after a day (you can change this), and then tells you to call. It never sends a new order.

**"What does it cost, what if it stops, can I switch it off, who do I call?"** See section 6, and you: you are the person the owner calls. The owner can stop it with one message.

5. **Money flow only.** Say who pays whom. Never say how much anyone will make or save.

## 7. Never say

| Phrase | Why | Say instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "It orders for you automatically." | Nothing is sent without the owner's yes | "It prepares the exact order and you say yes." |
| "The supplier confirmed, so it is delivered." | Their OK is not delivery | "They said they will supply it; you mark it when it arrives." |
| "It pays the supplier." | It pays nobody | "You pay as you do today." |
| "It will save you money." | No claims about savings | "It keeps your monthly limit." |
| "It always picks the cheapest supplier." | It does not compare quotes | "It orders from the supplier you name or pick." |

## 8. The setup visit

1. Write the supplier list with the owner: name, WhatsApp number, language, and whether each is approved. Ask for what is on the card "What to ask the shop for" (`sell/ask-card.en.md`); it also says what not to promise.
2. Write the catalogue: each item, supplier, unit (box, piece, bottle) and the last price. Ask the owner to check every price.
3. Set the limits with the owner: the most one order may be worth, and the month's budget.
4. Show the owner the card once, with test numbers, and have the owner tap Approve and Decline.
5. Say that suppliers' templates need Meta's approval, which takes time you do not control: give no start date.
6. Read the hand-check result together and sign the shop terms.

## 9. The first month

Check in during week 1 (any order held or asked about?). Read the first monthly view together once the report is built. Ask for one introduction, never as a reward. Then keep looking after it: tune the limits when the owner asks, add suppliers, update prices.

## 10. When something goes wrong

Stop the agent, call the owner before they find out, look at the record, own it. A supplier who got an order they should not have: the record shows the card, the digest and the owner's yes.

## 11. Be honest about what's true today

As of 17 November 2026 the Stock orders agent and its checks run against a stand-in WhatsApp only. No shop is live on it and no real supplier has been messaged. Not yet: paying the supplier, a real WhatsApp account or Meta's approval of the templates, a native speaker's review of the Hindi, Malayalam and Kannada wording, a saved store and the shop's page, a signed monthly report, comparing quotes. So: promise no start date, take no setup fee, and say plainly "it is not live anywhere yet".
