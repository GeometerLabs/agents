# Explanation: how the Bookings template v0 core was built, and why

3 November 2026. This explains the choices in `templates/commerce/bookings/`. It builds the first template of the product decision "Geometer: the product" (section 3) as laid out in the implementation plan (sections 1, 4 and 7); both live in the Geometer monorepo's `docs/` folder, not in this repository. What is intentionally left open is in [Deferred.md](Deferred.md).

> **5 November 2026: the shared code moved out (B30).** When the Fees template arrived, what both templates need moved into [`modules/`](../../../modules/README.md): language, understanding, payments (links, refunds, Guard's model), messaging, the record, report signing, the approvals relay, the shared limits, reminders, the crash-test frame and the stand-ins. This template imports them. Paths in this file that begin `src/adapters/live/`, `src/language/` and so on may now be under `modules/`; [`modules/Explanation.md`](../../../modules/Explanation.md) lists where each went and what stayed here. The hand check gives the same answers before and after (37 of 37 for Glow Salon, 32 of 32 for the rental).

## What was asked, and what v0 is

A Bookings agent for businesses that take a deposit and have a cancellation rule, with its limits, its record and a way to check it, running **locally, at zero cost, with no network and no npm dependencies**. It is the "Phase 0" prototype core: the same code is meant to run later against real services through Guard, and against the engine's simulated services for the crash test. So the design keeps the seams the plan names: adapters, pure limits, a record, one step per call.

Everything here runs against in-memory fakes. That is a stand-in for real services, not a claim about them.

## The decisions that matter

**1. "Confirmed" is a read, never a reply.** Every write (hold a slot, create a link, confirm the event, refund, send a message) is followed by a read from that service's own store, and only the read is recorded as `confirmed`. This is the central idea of the product ("a booking counts when the calendar's record shows it") and it is what catches the calendar that says ok and saves nothing. Each write is also preceded by a look: after a lost reply the agent finds its own earlier event, link or refund by the booking id instead of making a second one (the refund-desk pattern, applied everywhere).

**2. Payment events say "look", the payments record says what happened.** `payment_link.paid` and `payment.captured` describe the same payment, and either can arrive twice, late, or never. The agent counts payments from the link's own list, by payment id, so no combination of events can create a second deposit or lose one. When a hold is about to expire, the agent looks at the payments record first.

**3. The hold is a tentative calendar event.** The plan leaves open where a hold lives. Keeping it in the calendar means a hold is visible to every other booking and survives the agent losing its own state; confirming is an update from tentative to confirmed. The cost is one more calendar operation (`update`), which the plan's list (insert, get, list, delete) did not include.

**4. A payment after the hold expired re-checks the slot.** If the slot is still free, the agent holds it again and confirms. If not, it refunds the deposit (within the limit) and tells the owner. The customer paid in good faith, so the agent never keeps money for a booking it cannot give.

**5. The limits are pure, and each message is gated.** `src/limits.ts` has no side effects and reads nothing but its arguments, so Guard can run the same functions later. Every outgoing message passes through one function (`say`) that renders the templates from the record, runs the limits on the **final text**, sends only if none blocks, and reads the message back from the provider's outbox. "Booked" additionally requires `confirm-only-when-recorded`. Hours and double-booking are checked again whenever a booking is moved, at the customer's yes as well as at the request, because someone else may have taken the time in between.

**6. Templates only, and the check reads text like a customer.** No v0 message is model-written. Templates take their values from one `MessageFacts` object built from the booking and settings (only the menu carries every service's price; any other message may state only its own booking's service); the outgoing check (`src/language/check.ts`) reads the final text again from scratch (rupee amounts, dates with their weekday, times with their period word such as शाम, links, any leftover number) and blocks whatever the record cannot explain. It does not care how the text was made, so it will keep working when a model writes the free text around the templates. It compares values, not which label a value sits under: a ₹450 deposit where the record says ₹200 passes if ₹450 is also this booking's price (a known limit, in Deferred.md). Two tests keep templates and check in step: a drift test (every template, in every language, passes the check) and a mutation test (changing any one value in any template makes it fail, including swapping in another service's price; about 930 mutations are tried across the four languages).

**7. Dates have written rules.** "अगले मंगलवार" is the Tuesday of the next calendar week (Monday to Sunday). A bare weekday is the first one after today. "कल" is tomorrow in the business's time zone, not UTC. Every weekday phrase and every time without am/pm is read back with the full date, and nothing is held until the customer says yes. The rules are in the header of `modules/language/dates.ts` and in the README.

**8. STOP is strict.** After STOP, nothing goes to the customer: no acknowledgement, no reminder, no reply if they write again. The booking itself stays. Messages to the owner still go. Two rules cover it (`opt-out` for every message, `no-message-after-stop` for reminders) so either one alone would hold.

**9. The cancellation window means "enough notice".** Cancel at least `windowHours` before the start and the deposit is refunded, if it is within `refundLimitInr`. Cancel later and the owner decides by replying "approve b-3" or "deny b-3" (a refund within the limit; for one above the limit the question is Guard's, asked once with a code, see "Approvals on WhatsApp"). "Within the limit" includes the limit itself. The late-cancellation case uses the `refund-limit` rule id rather than inventing a tenth rule.

**10. One state per step, state in a store that is passed in.** `agent.handle(event)` takes one message, one payment event or one tick and returns. Conversation state lives in a `Store` handed to the agent, never in module variables, so every situation starts clean and the engine can run each step as a task. Inbound messages are marked seen when they arrive (a customer who is ignored can write again); payment events are marked seen only after they were processed (a failed step can be run again with the same event, and processing is idempotent anyway).

## How the checks were made, and why they should not be vacuous

- **Situations, not only unit tests.** `crash-test/situations.ts` holds 38 situations (27 at v0, then Malayalam and Kannada, then a customer who asks for more back than they paid, then the AI allowance, a model that is down, a button reply, and the monthly report; see below): the rows of plan 4.4 that fakes can express (1 to 14, with #3, #8 and #9 split into variants and #8 and #9 in both languages), plus a few the plan implies (a normal booking, cancellation inside the window, a refund above the limit, STOP, two customers on one slot, a conflict at the moment of the hold, outside opening hours, reschedule, a reschedule confirmed after the time was taken, reminders). Each one starts a fresh world, makes something go wrong on purpose, and judges **from the fakes' own records**: what the calendar holds, what the payments service holds, what the messaging outbox holds. The agent's return values are never consulted. The same situations back both the tests and `scripts/hand-check.ts`.
- **A mutation check by hand.** To see whether the situations can fail, 17 deliberate bugs were put into a scratch copy, one at a time (trust the calendar's reply, no inbound de-duplication, count every payment as a deposit, no look before creating a link, refunds always allowed, late cancellations refunded automatically, weekday phrases not confirmed, no double-booking check, hours not checked, "booked" without both confirmations, outgoing check switched off, and others). At first 14 were caught. Three were not: removing only the "I can't go lower" sentence (the quote still states ₹450; a check on that sentence was added), letting every message state every service's price (invisible to the situations because the templates never do; a unit test on the facts was added), and switching off only the `opt-out` gate (the `no-message-after-stop` gate still holds the reminders, which is the point of having two; a test that the record shows the opt-out decision was added). A review also found that a reschedule confirmed with "yes" skipped the slot rules; it is fixed, with a situation for it. One test regex had mistaken "is free" (the slot is available) for "free of charge". Checks that read the agent's own record (rather than the fakes) live in `test/record-decisions.test.ts`, apart from the situations.
- **A type check that is not part of the folder.** Node strips types without checking them. The code was checked once with TypeScript 5.9 borrowed read-only from another project's `node_modules` (`strict`, `erasableSyntaxOnly`, `verbatimModuleSyntax`, `noUnusedLocals`), with no errors. No tsconfig is committed, because it would need `@types/node` installed, and this folder has no dependencies.

## Demo mode (a partner shows it on the owner's phone)

Added 3 November 2026 in `demo/`. The product doc's partner journey asks for "a live demo on the shopkeeper's own phone" that passes the mom test. This is that, locally: no hosting, no network out, no dependencies, nothing in `src/` changed.

**1. The real agent, not a mock.** `createWorld` gives the agent, the stand-ins and a fake clock; the demo only adds a chat transcript (the fakes keep what was sent out, not what came in), the meaning of the buttons, and one-at-a-time handling. Because the agent has no locking (Deferred.md), every session runs its taps through a promise queue: three quick taps on "Pay" make one payment.

**2. The buttons are plain calls on the stand-ins.** "Pay" is `customerPays` on the link of the latest booking waiting for its deposit, followed by the payment provider's webhooks reaching the agent, exactly as the situations do. "Pay it twice" is two payments on one link (two phones), not the same webhook delivered twice. "1 hour" and "1 day" move the clock in steps of 15 minutes, each followed by a tick, because the agent sends only the latest due reminder per tick and a single big jump would skip one. Owner buttons send `approve b-1`, `deny b-1` or `retry b-1` from the owner's number, as the owner would type it; the command is shown in the owner's chat.

**3. The owner's lines are counted, not written.** Each line comes from the stand-ins' records or the agent's action record: "in your calendar, we checked" needs a "booked" message that really is in the outbox, a confirmed event on the calendar's own list at the booking's start, and a payment on the link. The ₹ amount of a refused discount is read from the price rule's decision. Lines about things that have not happened are rules, not results ("Nobody has asked for a discount yet. It never goes below your price."). The strings exist in English and Hindi; any other language shows English until a reviewed set is added to `demo/strings.ts`.

**4. The fake clock starts on today's real date at 10:00 shop time**, so "tomorrow" on the owner's phone is the real tomorrow and every suggestion is still ahead whatever the hour (starting at the real time of day would put an evening demo's "tomorrow 6pm" inside the 24-hour window, and "1 day passes" would then run past the booking instead of cancelling inside the window). Tests pin it to Tue 3 Nov 2026, 10:00. The suggestions under the chat are written from the shop's own first service, prices and opening days, and move to the next open day when tomorrow is closed.

**5. One shop per server.** The shop form replaces the shop for every device on this server (other open pages start again on their next request), so a partner can set it up on the laptop and open it on the phone. A failed form changes nothing. An untouched service keeps all its names, and untouched hours keep each day's own hours; a renamed service uses the new name in every language. The refund limit is raised to at least the deposit.

**6. Safety, because a laptop on Wi-Fi is a server.** It listens on the machine's private addresses and 127.0.0.1, never 0.0.0.0, and refuses any client that is not on a private network (even one a router forwards in). The Host header must be an address or a .local name (no DNS rebinding), a POST must be JSON and its Origin must match the page, the cookie is HttpOnly and SameSite=Strict and never chosen by the client, and bodies, messages, sessions and requests per device are capped. The page is served with a Content-Security-Policy that allows only its own nonced script and style; every piece of typed text reaches the page through `textContent`. The deposit link is shown as text, never a tappable link, because the fake mints real-looking `rzp.io` addresses. A test runs the page's real script against the real server with a stand-in for the browser's page and checks that no markup is ever made from typed text.

**Choices to review**
1. One shop for the whole server, not per phone (decision 5).
2. The clock starts on the real date, not a fixed day, so a script that says "tomorrow" fails when tomorrow is a closed day unless the suggestions are used.
3. "Pay it twice" is only offered while a deposit is waiting, or once more after it was paid.
4. The record layer ("What we did") is English only; it is for the person showing the demo.
5. The demo's screens use Hindi written without a native review.

## The demo in the browser (B42, added 4 November 2026)

"Build your first agent, in the browser": the partner demo above, running entirely in a visitor's page, with no server and no sign-in, for the website's `/start` page. It is also the base for a later "Try to break it" game. `mountDemo(element, options?)` draws three steps inside any element (the shop form, the chat with test money, what was checked); `scripts/build-web-demo.ts` builds it (`npm run build:web-demo`, output in `demo/web/dist/`, which git ignores). Nothing in `src/` changed, and no dependency was added.

**1. The same code, not a copy.** The build reads the same `.ts` files that run the live agent and the local-network demo, from where they are, and for each one (a) takes the types out with Node's own `module.stripTypeScriptTypes` in `strip` mode, which keeps every other character, and (b) changes only the names in `import ... from "x"`: `.ts` becomes `.js`, and `node:fs` and `node:crypto` point at two small stand-ins in `demo/web/shims/`. A test strips the types from each source file itself and checks that the built file equals it, apart from those names, for every file under `src/` and `demo/`. The build starts from the entry (`demo/web/mount.ts`) and follows imports, so the server files (`app.ts`, `page.ts`, `server.ts`) are not in it. Any other `node:` module, a package, or a computed `import()` stops the build.

**2. The two stand-ins.** Only three things in the template's reach are Node's: `readFileSync` (`src/harness.ts`, to read the example shop's settings), and `createHash` and `randomInt` from `node:crypto` (`modules/record/record.ts`, `modules/approvals/code.ts`).
- `shims/fs.ts` serves the files the build copied into `generated.js`. The build finds them by reading the code: a module that takes `readFileSync` from `node:fs` may call it only as `readFileSync(new URL("relative/path", import.meta.url), "utf8")`, otherwise the build stops. Today that is one file, `settings/glow-salon.example.json`. Anything not copied is `ENOENT`.
- `shims/crypto.ts` is SHA-256 written out in plain JavaScript (the constants worked out from the primes) and `randomInt` over `crypto.getRandomValues` without bias. It is plain JavaScript and not WebCrypto because the action record fingerprints each entry in `append`, synchronously (`modules/record/record.ts`), and WebCrypto answers later; using it would have meant making the record asynchronous, which is a change to the core and was not made. A test compares the stand-in with Node's SHA-256 on empty text, on lengths either side of the 55/56/63/64/65-byte block edges, and on Hindi, Malayalam, Kannada, `₹` and an emoji, and compares the built record's `digest` with the live one, so a browser's record has the same fingerprints as a live shop's.

**3. The behaviour is the existing page; the look is its own.** `demo/page.js` and the markup in `demo/page.ts` are shared by the local-network demo and the browser demo, so a tap does the same thing in both. The build puts the script, whole, in `generated.js` as `startPage(document, window, fetch, setTimeout, clearTimeout)` (the script uses those five names, so handing them in is all it needs; `demo.test.ts` already runs it against a stand-in for them), and the markup, taken by rendering `renderPage()` at build time and cutting out what is between `<body>` and the script. `mountDemo` hands the script a small `document` of its own: `getElementById` looks inside the demo, `lang` and `aria-busy` land on the demo's root element and not on the host page's `<html>` and `<body>`, `scrollTo` does nothing (a tab change must not scroll the host page), and `fetch` asks the demo in the page. The stylesheets are two: `demo/page.css` for the laptop demo (big plain words for a shop owner's phone, unchanged in look) and `demo/web/theme.css` for the browser (below). They style the same class names.

**4. One set of class names, all `geo-`.** To give a host something stable to restyle, every class in the markup and in what `page.js` draws was renamed to a `geo-` name (about 75 classes, a mechanical rename in `page.ts` and `page.js`; the laptop demo looks as before, which a browser check confirmed, and a test fails if a style sheet names a `geo-` class nothing uses). `demo/page.css` is also the base style of the deploy panel (`bundle/panel/views.ts` reads it and its pages use the old names: `btn`, `field`, `tab`, `wrap` ...), so each of its selectors now lists the old name and the `geo-` name, and nothing the panel uses was removed. The panel's tests pass. The old names can go from `page.css` when the panel moves to the `geo-` names (Deferred.md). The names (BEM-like: `geo-block`, `geo-block-part`, `geo-block--variant`):

The three steps.** The brief asked for steps and a "what was checked" panel; the demo had a form that opens over the chat, a Customer tab, an Owner tab and a hidden "What we did" tab. `mountDemo` adds the step bar and nothing else: each step only presses the page's own button (Your shop: "Set up for this shop"; Try it: the Customer tab; What was checked: the Owner tab) and reads what the page shows (`data-step` follows which one is open), so it cannot disagree with the page. The only colours written out in the file are those nine defaults, which a test checks (no hex, `rgb()`, `hsl()` or colour name anywhere else). **Dark mode is the host changing the tokens**: `demo/web/index.html` does it with a check box and ten lines of CSS, and a browser check showed the whole demo follow. The look: 1px hairlines, radii around 10px, 14 to 17px text (15px base) with 12px only for meta text (the two labels, the clock, times, pills, record heads and refs), no shadows and no heavy boxes (the owner's lines are rows with hairline dividers, the record is a list), the labels one quiet strip at the top.

**7. Scoped, and a host's element rules do not leak in.** Every rule in `theme.css` is under `.geo-demo` (a test checks each selector). A class prefix keeps our rules out of the host page but does nothing against the host's own rules for bare elements coming in (in a first browser check a host's `p { color: ... }` reached the chat bubbles). So the root is `all: initial` (inherited text styles from the host stop at the edge) and the elements inside start from the browser's own look (`all: revert` under `.geo-demo.geo-demo`, at low weight), then the theme applies. A host rule with a class and an element in it, or two classes, still out-ranks that; use the tokens. The demo is in the page's own tree, not a shadow root: a shadow root would have stopped all leaks but would have made the class names above useless to the page, and the brief was for a page that can restyle it. The stylesheet is added once per page as a constructed sheet (`document.adoptedStyleSheets`), which is not an inline `<style>`, so a page whose policy refuses inline styles still gets it (a `<style>` in `<head>` is the fallback). Every `id` in the markup gets a prefix that differs for each mount, so ids cannot clash with the host page's.

**8. The two labels, always on screen.** The page's own banner "Demo · test money · nothing real is sent" (it rewrites its words in the shop's language) and, in its own element beside it, "In this demo the template's own code checks the limit. On a live shop, Guard holds it outside the AI." Both sit in one quiet strip at the top of the frame, outside what scrolls, and stay in every step. The second label is English only for now.

**9. It opens on step 1** (`startWith: "form"`, the default), the shop form filled in with a made-up salon (Glow Salon, the example shop), so the visitor can build their own or press "Try it" and talk to the salon at once: name, up to four services with prices and minutes, deposit, hours and days, language. "Rebuild the demo for this shop" starts the chat with the shop's own menu. `startWith: "chat"` opens step 2. The form is the existing one, so the deposit rule is the deposit amount; the hold time and cancellation window are the example shop's.

**10. The actions are shared, not rewritten.** The `switch` in `demo/app.ts` that ran each tap (say, pay, time, cancel, owner, reset, shop) with its checks moved into `demo/api.ts` (`runAction`, `demoStart`, `HttpError`), and `app.ts` calls it; the server keeps the cookie, origin, host, address and rate checks. `demo/web/local.ts` calls the same `runAction` and the same `buildState`, and returns the answers in the shape of `fetch`. So a visitor's tap does exactly what a tap on the laptop demo does, with the same caps (300 characters, 200 messages, 600 taps). `local.ts` has no page code, so a test (or the later game) can drive the demo with no browser. The world is in the page's memory: one shop and one world for each `mountDemo` call, lost when the page closes. No request is made, nothing is stored (no cookies, no local storage), and the page cannot tell anyone anything. `mountDemo` writes markup once, with `template.innerHTML`, from the build's fixed markup with ids added; everything a visitor types reaches the page through `textContent`, as in the local demo.

**11. Tests** (`test/web-demo.test.ts`, each builds into a temp folder first, so `npm test` needs no earlier build): no file in the build imports a `node:` module, a package or a file that is not there, and none uses `require`, `process` or `Buffer`; every file under `src/` and `demo/` equals its source with only types and import names changed; the SHA-256 and `randomInt` stand-ins; the file stand-in serves the example shop and refuses anything else; a scripted session through the built modules (book a slot with a ₹200 deposit and pay, read the calendar's own list; ask for ₹300 and for free and see the price rule block it, with the link still for the deposit; rebuild the shop and see the same refusal at its own price; a form with a mistake changes nothing); the labels and the markup (every id the script looks for is there); every style rule is under the root class; every colour is a token default and the tokens are documented in the header of `theme.css`; the class names (both style sheets name only classes the demo has, and every class the demo has is styled or listed as a hook); the build refuses to write into a folder it did not make. One existing test changed: "the demo makes no network calls of its own" read every entry of `demo/` as a file, which a `demo/web/` folder breaks, so it now reads files only (`web/` has its own checks).

**12. Reply buttons and lists (5 Nov).** The demo no longer sends plain text. `demo/session.ts` builds its world with the stand-in WhatsApp as it is for the live agent, so the agent's replies come as the real interactive messages. The bubble is the message's **body**, and under it `page.js` draws the options the way WhatsApp does: up to three reply buttons, or a list as one button (its own label, "Pick a service") that opens the rows (title and description, with "Talk to the owner" last). A tap goes to the new `/api/tap` with the option's id (`svc:<id>`, `slot:<ms>`, `yes`, `no`, `cancel`, `cancel_yes`, `keep`, `owner`, `menu`: the ids the rule step maps, so no model is asked). The id is checked to be one the agent put on the screen (a customer can scroll up and tap an older one, as on WhatsApp; the older options are dimmed, not disabled), the customer's bubble shows the option's **title** as WhatsApp does, and the record shows `via=button` or `via=list`. Buttons are real `<button>`s, set with `textContent`, disabled while a tap is being handled, and the list's button says `aria-expanded`. It runs in both demos (the page and `api.ts` are shared). New classes: `geo-choices` (+ `--old`), `geo-choice` (+ `--list`), `geo-choice-title`, `geo-choice-desc`, `geo-rows`, `geo-row`, in `theme.css` and `page.css`. The scripted month keeps plain text. A booking can now be made, held, paid for and cancelled by taps alone (`web-demo.test.ts` does it through the built modules).

**Size, 5 Nov.** The build was 56 files, 559,576 bytes, 156,704 gzipped (each file alone, as the website's audit adds them up; its cap is 160 KiB = 163,840). It is now **52 files, 548,240 bytes, 151,332 gzipped**: the buttons cost about 1,300 gzipped (`page.js` and the style rules in `generated.js`, `session.ts`, `api.ts`), the pause on a failing step about 2,300 (`ai-state.ts`, `reader.ts`; mostly its comments), and Bookings' reading took `chosenProbability` from `modules/understanding/model.ts` instead of the router, so the router, the price table, `redact` and the System One client (about 9,000 gzipped) are no longer in a demo that asks no model.

**Choices to review**
1. It opens on the shop form, not the chat (decision 9). Pass `startWith: "chat"` on the page if the first thing a visitor should see is a conversation.
2. Light DOM, not a shadow root (decision 7): the page can restyle by class name and token, and a host rule can in rare cases still reach in. If leaks matter more than restyling, a shadow root is a small change in `mount.ts`, and the token names would stay.

5. The theme needs `color-mix()`, `:has()` and `dvh` (any browser since early 2024). The constructed stylesheet should pass a policy that refuses inline styles (checked in Chrome against `style-src 'self'`); `innerHTML` still needs a page that does not require Trusted Types. Neither was checked against the website's own policy.
6. The build output was about 411 KB (about 113 KB gzipped) in 35 files when this was written; it is 548 KB (151 KB gzipped) in 52 files now (point 12), loaded as separate modules. One file, minified, would be smaller and one request; it needs a minifier, which is a dependency.

## Malayalam and Kannada (added 3 November 2026)

Two more customer languages, `ml` and `kn`, on the same rules: every amount, date, time, service and link is rendered from the record, the outgoing check still fails closed, and a refused haggle states the price once. Everything below was written by a model, not a native speaker: [REVIEW-ml.md](REVIEW-ml.md) and [REVIEW-kn.md](REVIEW-kn.md) list every line for review, and no real shop should use these languages before it is done (Deferred.md).

**How the packs were added.** `Language` gained `"ml" | "kn"`, and every table keyed by language is a `Record<Language, …>`, so the type system refuses to build without a template for every message id, a name for every weekday and month, and a day-part table. A runtime test says the same, for a build that skips the type check. The date, time and weekday names are in `format.ts`, the messages in `templates.ts`, and the words customers type (the parser's Malayalam and Kannada) are plain lists in `modules/language/words.ts`, one place that `dates.ts`, `rule-parser.ts` and the review sheet all read.

**Decisions.**

1. **No ending is ever glued to a service name or a business name.** Malayalam and Kannada put the case ending on the noun and the ending depends on the noun's last letter ("ഫേഷ്യൽ" gives "ഫേഷ്യലിന്", "ഹെയർകട്ട്" gives "ഹെയർകട്ടിന്"), and a shop chooses its own names. So the templates use shapes that need none: "ഹെയർകട്ട് വില ₹450 ആണ്", "ಹೇರ್ ಕಟ್ ಬೆಲೆ ₹450", "ബുക്കിംഗ് റദ്ദാക്കി (ചൊവ്വ, 10 നവംബർ, വൈകുന്നേരം 6:00)". The slot is either a sentence's subject or in brackets after a label ("പുതിയ സമയം: …"), never followed by a suffix. The one place a suffix touches a name is a hyphen after the business name ("Glow Salon-ലേക്ക്"), the way people type it; that is the first thing for a reviewer to look at.
2. **Dates and times.** Date as weekday, day, month ("ചൊവ്വ, 10 നവംബർ" / "ಮಂಗಳವಾರ, 10 ನವೆಂಬರ್"). Time as a word for the part of the day, then the 12-hour clock time ("വൈകുന്നേരം 6:00" / "ಸಂಜೆ 6:00"), with the same hour limits as Hindi: morning 5 to 12, noon 12 to 4, evening 4 to 8, night otherwise. Words: രാവിലെ, ഉച്ചയ്ക്ക്, വൈകുന്നേരം, രാത്രി and ಬೆಳಿಗ್ಗೆ, ಮಧ್ಯಾಹ್ನ, ಸಂಜೆ, ರಾತ್ರಿ. The period word is part of the time the check reads, so "രാവിലെ 6:00" for a 6 pm booking is a wrong time and is blocked. The Hindi "बजे" has no counterpart; "6:00" is clear without it.
3. **The deposit is "അഡ്വാൻസ്" and "ಮುಂಗಡ ಹಣ"** (the advance, the everyday word in both states), the owner is "ഉടമ" and "ಮಾಲೀಕರು". Replies the customer is asked to write are "ശരി" and "ಹೌದು", both understood.
4. **One text form for matching.** `plain()` now also turns Malayalam and Kannada digits into 0-9, removes zero-width joiners and non-joiners (Kannada "ಹೇರ್‌ಕಟ್" is typed with one; it only changes how the letter is drawn), and writes old-style Malayalam chillus (consonant + virama + joiner) as the one-letter forms. Both the customer's text and every word list go through it, so they meet.
5. **The check.** It builds its date and time patterns from the same tables as the formatters (the weekday and month names of all four languages, the day-part words of the three that have them), so it cannot read a form the formatters do not write. Spelled-out hundreds and thousands are caught inside Malayalam and Kannada words too, because those languages join number words ("ഇരുന്നൂറ്" is 200, "ಇನ್ನೂರು" is 200). The "record shows" text in a problem is picked by the script of the offending word.
6. **The parser reads endings.** A word in Malayalam or Kannada letters also matches when more letters follow it ("നാളെയ്ക്ക്", "ಮಂಗಳವಾರದಂದು", "ಫೇಶಿಯಲ್‌ಗೆ"); a final virama is dropped from the stem, and a Malayalam chillu is allowed to be its plain consonant ("ഫേഷ്യൽ" matches "ഫേഷ്യലിന്"). Exact lists (yes, no, hello, month names, "6 മണി") do not, because they are short and ordinary. Known traps are handled by name: "ഇന്നലെ" (yesterday) begins like "ഇന്ന്" (today), so yesterday words are taken out before looking for a date; "6 മണിക്കൂർ" is six hours, not six o'clock; Kannada "300ಕ್ಕೆ" is a price but "6ಕ್ಕೆ" is "at 6", told apart by size (a price is 25 or more, a clock hour is 1 to 12), with the emphatic spelling "300ಕ್ಕೇ" read the same; and a written date's day is blanked out before either rule runs, so "ನವೆಂಬರ್ 10ಕ್ಕೆ" (for 10 November) is the 10th, not ten o'clock, and "ನವೆಂಬರ್ 28ಕ್ಕೆ" is not an offer of ₹28. The price rules now take the first match that is big enough instead of the first match, so "ಸಂಜೆ 6ಕ್ಕೆ ... 300ಕ್ಕೆ" finds the 300. Dates follow the Hindi rules exactly, including "next Tuesday is the Tuesday of next week", and the full date is read back and confirmed ("വരുന്ന ചൊവ്വാഴ്ച" and "ಬರುವ ಮಂಗಳವಾರ", the coming Tuesday, are a plain weekday).
7. **The language is read from the script first** (Devanagari, Malayalam, Kannada), then from Latin-letter words ("mani", "venam", "gante", "beku"). "nale" and "naale" are tomorrow in both languages, so alone they decide nothing; with "mani" or "gante" they do. A bare yes or no keeps the language the customer was using.
8. **The review sheets are generated, and tested.** `scripts/review-sheet.ts` writes one table per language from the templates, formatters and word lists, with the English template as the meaning and an empty column for the reviewer. It also lists the exact customer sentences the hand check types, because those sentences are the evidence that real phrasing works and a native reader should be able to say if they are not how people write. A test fails if the committed sheet is missing a template, a word or a sample sentence, or has lost its warning line, so the sheet cannot fall behind the code while reviewers are filling it in.

**How it was checked.** The drift and mutation tests run over all four languages (930 mutations: amounts, hours, minutes, day-part words, weekdays, months, days, links, hold and notice times). Each of the four crash-test situations that matter for a customer in another language now also runs in Malayalam and Kannada: a normal booking, a price below the floor (the price is stated once), a trick message, and an ambiguous date; plus a late cancellation where the owner reads the same language and answers with `approve b-1`. All judged from the fakes' own records. To see whether those situations can fail, two deliberate bugs were put in and both were caught (a Kannada quote that repeated the price; a Malayalam month table with two months swapped). A review of the first version found two Kannada holes the situations had not caught, both now fixed with tests: the "push again" sentence used the emphatic ೇ and was read as a bare "no" (the situation passed because nothing looked at the second reply; every price situation now checks that the second reply re-sends the held slot's link), and a written date followed by ಕ್ಕೆ was read as an hour or a price. The code was type-checked with the same borrowed TypeScript 5.9 as before, no errors.

**Size.** `src/` grew from 2,893 to 3,239 lines (about 350: the two sets of templates are 100, `words.ts` 144, the rest is in the parser and the check); tests, situations and scripts from 1,657 to 2,315 (the review sheet script and its test are 250 of that). Tests: 117 to 160. Situations: 27 to 33. Checks inside them: 130 to 196. Still no dependencies.

## Running it as a server (added 3 November 2026)

`server/` runs one client's agent live (implementation plan sections 1 and 6, design rules 1 to 7): every incoming event is saved as a task before the provider is answered, tasks are handled one at a time, the agent's state survives a restart, and a clock ticks so holds expire and reminders go out. Plain Node, `node:sqlite`, `node:http`, no dependencies. The agent itself is unchanged; the one change in `src/` is described in decision 9.

**Files.** `store.ts` (the SQLite file: tasks, state snapshot, record, WhatsApp windows), `snapshot.ts` (the agent's store as JSON), `events.ts` (what the inbox may send, and the key that makes a redelivery a no-op), `runtime.ts` (one step, saved whole), `tasks.ts` (the serial queue, retries, resume), `ticker.ts`, `server.ts` (HTTP), `config.ts` (the client folder and its pin), `services.ts` (stand-ins or live adapters), `start.ts` and `main.ts` (start a client; the command line).

**1. A step is saved whole or not at all.** For every step the runtime builds a fresh store and record from the last saved state, builds a fresh agent over them, lets the agent handle the event (in memory), and then saves in ONE transaction: the new snapshot, the record entries the step made, and the task's "done" mark. Only after that commit does it treat the new state as the saved one. If the agent throws, or the save fails, nothing was saved and nothing in memory is reused, so there is nothing to undo: the retry starts from the last saved state by construction. The agent keeps nothing of its own between steps, so rebuilding it costs a JSON parse. Ticks go through the same save (they expire holds and send reminders, so they change the state), except a tick that changed nothing, which writes nothing.

**2. What happens when the process dies in the middle of a step.** If the process dies in the middle of a step, the step re-runs from the last saved snapshot. The agent's look-before-retry design makes the calendar and payment-link writes safe to repeat (it finds its own hold and link by the booking id, which is why `nextBooking` is saved), and a refund is looked up by its receipt before it is made again, but a WhatsApp message might go out twice. A WhatsApp send has nothing to look it up by, and the window is the length of one step: from its first send until its save, so the customer's quote, or the message to the owner, can arrive twice. Two more things to know. The record entries of the cut-off run are lost with it (they were only in memory), so the record shows the re-run, not the interrupted try; the services' own records keep whatever landed, and the re-run's read-backs show it. `test/runtime.test.ts` ("a step cut off in the middle") shows both sides: after the re-run there is one hold and one link, and the customer has the quote twice. A possible fix, writing "about to send" before each send, is in Deferred.md.

**3. Saved before the 200.** `POST /internal/events` answers 200 only after `INSERT OR IGNORE` of every event in one transaction, with `synchronous = FULL` so the commit is on disk even if the power goes. The task's key is the event's own id with its kind in front (`message:<wamid>`, `payment_event:<evt>`), so a redelivery is `{"duplicates": 1}` and creates nothing, even after the task is done or the process was restarted. The whole request is checked first and refused whole (400) if any event is malformed; a tick from outside is refused. The same transaction notes when the sender last wrote, because the reply to that very message needs the WhatsApp window open when its step runs, even a retry minutes later. The window is the later of the old and new time, never later than now.

**4. One at a time, in order; retries; failure.** One promise chain is the queue. A saved task is taken in the order it arrived; a tick takes its turn in the same chain, and is skipped if one is already waiting, so ticks do not pile up behind a slow step. A step that throws is tried again after 1 s, 5 s and 30 s (four tries), in place, so the order of a customer's messages is kept; then the task is marked `failed` with its error and the queue goes on. The attempt is counted and saved before the step runs, so a step that kills the process each time is marked failed at the next start instead of looping across restarts. Failures show in `/status` (the failed count and the time of the last failure); a failed task does not make `/readyz` fail, because restarting would not help it. A tick that throws is logged and not retried; the next one comes in 30 s.

**5. What is stored.** `PRAGMA user_version` holds the schema version and migrations run in order (a newer file than the code is refused in plain words). `record` is append-only, enforced by the database with two triggers, and its entries keep their digests (the record is kept in memory as well, because the limits read it on every step). Message text is not kept once a task is done: the task's payload is emptied and the record holds only digests, which is the default in plan 4.5. A task that failed keeps its payload, to be looked at.

**6. The endpoints.** The internal token is compared in constant time (both sides hashed with SHA-256 first, so a short guess and a long one take the same time); it comes from a file named in `INTERNAL_TOKEN_FILE`, trimmed, and at least 16 characters, never from an argument or a settings file. The body limit is 256 KiB (checked on the declared length and again as the body arrives; 413 and the connection is closed), at most 100 events a request. `/status` is counts only: bookings by state, tasks pending, done and failed, the oldest pending task's time, the last step and last failure times, the record length and the template version. A test checks that it carries no service name, business name, time, phone number or link. It listens on 127.0.0.1 unless told otherwise; the TLS entry and the private network are the deploy bundle's.

**7. The client folder and its pin.** `settings.json` is the existing format; `runtime.json` says how this agent runs (mode, host, port, data folder, tick seconds, and for live the base URL of the Guard in front of each service); `pathway.lock` pins `bookings@<version>`. The version is the one in `package.json` (0.1.0 now, so a version change must change the example's lock; a test fails otherwise). A different pin is refused before anything is opened, and the message names both versions. A lock that lists modules is refused too, since this runtime cannot load them. Every mistake in the folder is said in plain words, all at once.

**8. Fakes and live.** Fakes mode wires the in-memory stand-ins and prints what the stand-in WhatsApp is asked to send. They live in memory, so after a restart the saved bookings point at events that no longer exist (the server warns when the data folder is not new). Live mode loads `src/adapters/live/index.ts` by dynamic import, only then, and passes it the `services` block as it is plus the clock and a `WindowStore` backed by the window table. If that file is not there (as on this branch) it stops with a plain message. The tests check the wiring against a stand-in module, never the real adapters.

**9. The one change in `src/`.** `createRecord(clock, saved = [])` takes the entries an earlier run already wrote and continues after them. The record keeps only a digest of each entry's inputs, so a restart cannot rebuild it by replaying `append`; the alternative was a second copy of the record's queries in `server/`, which could drift from the first. Nothing else in `src/` changed, and the 183 earlier tests and the hand check ("Held 33 of 33") are untouched. `package.json` gained `"version": "0.1.0"` and `npm run serve`.

**10. A seam for tests.** `createRuntime` takes an optional `aroundStep(event, run)` that wraps every agent step. The tests use it to hold a step open (to show the 200 comes while the step is still running), to count steps that overlap (none), and to make a step fail after the agent has done its work. The agent itself swallows most service errors, so a real failure is hard to produce otherwise. Nothing in production sets it.

**Choices to review**
1. Message text is dropped from a task once it is done (decision 5). Keeping it would help debugging and replay; it would also make every customer message a stored one, against plan 4.5's default. A per-client retention setting would let a builder choose.
2. Retries wait in place (1 s, 5 s, 30 s), so a broken message delays the customers behind it by up to 36 s. The other choice, moving it to the back of the queue, would reorder a customer's messages.
3. A step that fails every time is marked failed and the customer gets no answer. Someone has to look at `failed` in the status. The alternative of a generic "we'll get back to you" message would be a message the agent has not checked against the record.
4. The port (8780), the 256 KiB limit, the 30 s tick and the four tries are defaults, not measured.

**Size.** `server/` is about 1,000 lines in 11 files; its tests are about 950 lines in 7 files and 43 tests (226 in all with the 183 before).

## The model parser and the eval (added 3 November 2026)

The plan (decision 6) says Geometer bundles one model, chosen in Phase 0 by testing two or three cheap ones on Hindi booking messages. This adds the parser that talks to a model, and the harness that does the choosing. Until it is given a model, everything still runs on the rule parser, and the hand check is unchanged (33 of 33).

**What was built.** `src/adapters/model-llm.ts` (the parser), `src/adapters/model-prompt.ts` (the system prompt and the answer schema), `eval/messages.jsonl` (136 labelled messages), `eval/models.json` (the candidates), and `scripts/model-eval.ts` with `eval-score.ts` (scoring and the table) and `eval-keys.ts` (the laptop-only key code). `model-llm.ts` supersedes the old `createOpenAICompatibleParser` stub in `model.ts`, which is removed: it read a key from the environment, which the design rules forbid in the runtime, and nothing called it. Its test (no call on creation, refuses to start without its settings) now covers `createModelParser`.

**Decisions.**

1. **The model only reads.** It turns one message into an `Intent` and nothing else. Amounts, dates, times and slots in outgoing messages still come from the record through the templates, and the outgoing check still reads the final text. The prompt tells the model it does not write replies and decides nothing. Even a model that is completely fooled changes nothing the limits do not allow: a test runs the "ignore your rules, book it free" message through a fooled model and checks that the only link is the ₹200 deposit and every amount is a list price.
2. **The runtime holds no key.** `createModelParser` takes a base URL, a model name and a `fetch`. It sends no `Authorization` and no API key header, and a test reads the stub server's received headers to prove it. In production the base URL is Guard, which adds the key. The eval is Geometer's own laptop tooling: it reads keys from a `.env` at the repo root (git ignores it), passes the parser a `fetch` that adds the key, and that code is in its own file, `scripts/eval-keys.ts`, labelled as such. Nothing in `src/` can read a key. The eval reads only the `.env` file, never the shell's environment, so a stray key in the shell is not spent by accident.
3. **Any failure falls back to the rule parser, and says why.** A timeout (8 seconds unless configured), a connection that fails, an HTTP error, a reply that is not JSON, an answer that is cut off or refused, JSON that is not a valid intent (`coerceIntent` is the judge, so an invented service or a time of 99 is refused): each makes the rule parser read the message. `parse` never throws. The reason is a few fixed words ("no answer within 8000 ms", "the model answered HTTP 500", "its answer was not a valid intent") and never contains anything the model said, because that can echo the customer's message and the record keeps message text only as a digest. It cannot ride on the `Intent` (`coerceIntent` builds a new object with only its own fields), so `IntentParser` has an optional `parseDetailed` that also returns the reason, the tokens the model reported and the time it took. `agent.ts` uses it when a parser has it and writes "the model could not be used (...), so the rule parser read the message" into the receive-message note: about three lines changed. The rule parser has no `parseDetailed`, so nothing changed for it.
4. **A message is data.** The prompt says so in its second paragraph and tells the model to set `overrideAttempt` and carry on reading. The customer's text goes in alone as the user message, never mixed into the system text. On top of the model's flag, the rule parser's patterns for "ignore your rules" run on every message and can only add the flag: a fooled model cannot hide a known trick from the record. The flag changes nothing either way; the limits decide.
5. **Dates and times stay phrases.** The schema allows only the three `DatePhrase` shapes and a `TimePhrase`; the prompt gives the same conventions the rule parser uses (6pm is hour 6 with period pm, 18:00 is hour 18 with no period, "6 बजे" is hour 6 with no period, "next" and "this" and a plain weekday are different phrases) and never tells the model today's date or a year, so it cannot resolve one. `dates.ts` resolves them as before. One repair: a model that writes `{hour: 18, period: "pm"}` is meant 18:00, and `resolveTime` would turn that into hour 30, so the period is dropped when the hour is outside 1 to 12, and `coerceIntent` now refuses that combination from any parser.
6. **One schema, two wires.** Every field is required and "not said" is null, because OpenAI's strict mode wants every field required, and every object is closed (`additionalProperties: false`) and no range is used (min and max), because Anthropic's structured outputs want that. The parser drops the nulls and the fields that do not belong to a phrase's kind before `coerceIntent` sees the answer. The `serviceId` enum is this shop's own ids. On the OpenAI-compatible wire `response_format` is `json_schema` (strict) or, for a provider without schemas, `json_object` (the prompt already contains the word JSON, which that mode requires). `extraBody` is merged over the request for a provider's own quirks (`max_completion_tokens`, `reasoning_effort`, a null `temperature`). Models that think out loud inside `<think>` tags or wrap the JSON in a code fence are tolerated.
7. **The Anthropic wire.** `POST <baseUrl>/v1/messages` with `anthropic-version: 2026-10-21`, `output_config.format` of type `json_schema` for the answer, no `temperature` (that API version refuses it) and no forced tool choice (the newer models refuse it), and an optional `output_config.effort` (`low` suits reading a short message). The answer is the first `text` block, because a thinking model may put a thinking block first. A `refusal` or `max_tokens` stop is a fallback. This was written from the Claude API skill's raw-HTTP reference, because the template has no dependencies.
8. **The eval labels come from meaning.** Every `expected` was written by hand from what the message says, in the rule parser's phrase conventions, and not generated by running the rule parser (that would score the baseline at 100 percent by construction). Each place the rule parser disagreed was then looked at: all 19 were gaps in the parser (typos, spelled-out hours, "shift", "സൗജന്യമായി", a polite STOP, tricks it has no pattern for), not label mistakes. Every line has a one-line `note` for the reviewer, and the file opens with a comment line saying it is machine-written and needs native review. `loadMessages` refuses a label that is not a valid `Intent` for the shop.
9. **How an answer is scored.** An answer is right when six fields are right: kind, serviceId, date, time, offerInr, overrideAttempt (absent means false). 6 pm and 18:00 are the same time, but "6" with no am/pm is not either of them (it still has to be confirmed with the customer). `language` is scored separately and is not in the total, because a bare "ok" or a code-mixed message has no clean label. A message the model could not answer is scored as the rule parser's answer, because that is what a shop would get, and its own row ("only where the model answered") shows the model alone next to the fallback rate. Latency counts every call, including the ones that timed out. `--max-messages N` sends N messages taken in turn from the four languages, so a cheap run still tests all of them. The `overrideAttempt` row is the parser as the agent sees it, not each model alone: the rule parser's patterns for known tricks are OR-ed in (decision 4), so that row cannot tell models apart on tricks those patterns already know. Calls are made one at a time, so latency is not distorted and a low rate limit is respected (`pauseMs` per candidate).

**The baseline.** The rule parser gets all six fields right on 117 of the 136 messages (86.0 percent): Hindi 87.9, English 83.8, Malayalam 81.8, Kannada 90.9. By field: kind 95.6, serviceId 100, date 99.3, time 95.6, offerInr 98.5, overrideAttempt 97.1. What it misses is what a model should win: typos ("tommorow", "മണിക്"), hours spelled out ("छह बजे", "ആറ് മണിക്ക്"), a request to move a booking said loosely, a polite STOP, a longer "no", tricks without the words it knows, and "സൗജന്യമായി".

The crash-test-style checks for situation 13 and 9 with a model are in the same file, not in `crash-test/situations.ts`: adding situations would change the hand check's count and open sockets from it. ## Live services (added 3 November 2026)

Phase 0 of the implementation plan: the code that talks to the real wire formats of Razorpay, Google Calendar and WhatsApp, HTTP stand-ins that speak the same formats, and the webhook inbox. Design rules 1, 3, 6 and 7 of the plan are what shaped it. Nothing here has talked to a real account; it was checked against the providers' own sample payloads and against the stand-ins over real HTTP. What is left is in [Deferred.md](Deferred.md), "Live services".

**What is where.** `src/adapters/live/` (the three adapters, `http.ts`, `webhooks.ts`, and `index.ts` with `createLiveAdapters`), `src/language/meta-templates.ts` and `whatsapp-templates.json` (the templates), `inbox/` (its own process), `standins/` (the stand-ins, and `dev-forwarder.ts`).

**1. No keys, one base URL each.** Every live adapter is `create...({ baseUrl, ... })` and sends no Authorization header. In production the base URL is a Guard instance in front of that one service and Guard adds the credential; in a test it is a stand-in; for the Phase 0 prototype it is the dev forwarder. Same code in all three. A base URL that holds a user name or password is refused at start, and the base URL is joined to paths by plain concatenation so a path prefix (`http://127.0.0.1:8791/razorpay`) is kept.

**2. The seam stays; errors are the existing ones.** The adapters implement `Payments`, `Calendar` and `Messaging` unchanged and raise the existing errors. A call with no reply (timeout after 10 s by default, a connection cut after the request left, a 2xx whose body cannot be read) is a `TimeoutError`, because it may have happened and the agent looks before it retries. A refused connection or unknown host is an `ApiError(503)`, because nothing was sent. Any other non-2xx is an `ApiError` with that status. Google's 409 is a `ConflictError`; WhatsApp's code 131047 (and the local window check) is a `WindowClosedError`. Five small additions were needed and are the only changes to the seam: `Refund.status` may be `"pending"` (Razorpay's word for accepted, not yet paid out; a `failed` refund is not a refund and is never listed), `PaymentEvent.payload.payment.link_id` is optional, `listPaymentLinks` accepts `payment_id`, `TemplateMessage` has optional `parameters`, and `OutboundMessage` has an optional `acceptedAs`. The 183 tests did not need to change, but the fake side did move a little: `createFakePayments.listPaymentLinks` filters by `payment_id`, the harness approves the seven new template names, and outside the 24-hour window the fake's outbox now records the real template name (with the same text) for six messages (`booked`, `couldnt_confirm`, `rescheduled`, `refund_approved`, `duplicate_refund`, `owner_refund_needs_approval`) instead of `booking_update` or `owner_notice`. Only `booking_reminder` is asserted anywhere, and it keeps its name. `need`, `slot` and `inr` are now exported from `language/templates.ts`.

**3. Razorpay (Payment Links v1; docs cited in `razorpay.ts`).**
- A payment inside a fetched link is `{ payment_id, amount, method, status, created_at }`: no `id`, no `amount_refunded`. `fetchPaymentLink` therefore reads each payment from `GET /v1/payments/{id}` (one or two calls) so the refund figure the agent uses for an extra payment is real. A payment that cannot be read keeps what the link said.
- A list shows `payments: []` even on a paid link (the docs' own example). The agent never reads payments from a list, and the stand-in reproduces that on purpose.
- A missing id is HTTP **400** "The id provided does not exist", not 404; fetches return `null` for it.
- A duplicate `reference_id` is 400 "payment link creation with reference ID already attempted": an `ApiError`, and `ensureLink` already lists by `reference_id` before it tries again.
- `partially_paid` reads as paid (we never set `accept_partial`), `expired` as cancelled. `captured` or `refunded` payments count as captured; an authorised or failed payment is not a deposit.
- **A real `payment.captured` does not say which link it paid.** The body has the payment and its order, not the link (`payment_link.paid` has the link). A second payment on an already-paid link may produce only `payment.captured`, so without help the duplicate deposit would be ignored as "no booking has this link". The agent now asks the payments service which link the payment paid (`GET /payment_links?payment_id=`, which Razorpay documents). The stand-in's `payment.captured` omits the link on purpose, and a test covers it end to end.
- The event id is the `x-razorpay-event-id` header, not in the body. Without the header the id is a digest of the body, which a retry repeats.

**4. Google Calendar (v3; docs cited in `google-calendar.ts`).** The booking id and hold expiry live in `extendedProperties.private` and are found again with `privateExtendedProperty=bookingRef=...`. `timeMin` bounds an event's end and `timeMax` its start (both exclusive), so "overlaps [start, end)" is exactly one `events.list` with `singleEvents=true`, following `nextPageToken`. The patch page says nothing about how `extendedProperties` merge, so `update` reads the event first and sends the booking id back with the change; a cleared hold is sent as `null`, which is how Google removes a private property. A cancelled event reads as gone (404, 410 and a `status: "cancelled"` body all give `null`), deleting an already deleted event succeeds, and a free/busy answer with errors raises instead of reading as "free". Events the owner made have no booking id and read as `"foreign"`; ones marked "free" block nothing; all-day events use the calendar's own time zone from the list answer. Times are written in UTC. Google never refuses an overlapping insert, so there is a short race between the agent's look at conflicts and its insert (Deferred.md).

**5. WhatsApp (Cloud API shape, Graph v26.0; docs cited in `whatsapp.ts`).** The provider is not chosen. The adapter is thin and written for Meta's shape so a provider (Gupshup, AiSensy, Interakt, 360dialog) is one more small file that builds its own request and reads its own reply. The 24-hour window has no API, so `isWindowOpen` reads the runtime's own record of when each customer last wrote (`WindowStore`), and a closed window is refused before any call. **WhatsApp has no endpoint to read a sent message back**, so `getMessage` returns the local copy of what the send call accepted, keyed by the wamid, and a live "confirmed" means *accepted by WhatsApp*, not *delivered*. The reply's `message_status` is kept (`acceptedAs`); `paused` is refused. After a restart the copy is gone and the read-back says "unconfirmed". Delivery statuses arrive as webhooks and are ignored for now.

**6. Templates.** Outside the 24-hour window WhatsApp carries only an approved template. Meta will not approve a template that is only a variable, and a parameter cannot hold line breaks, so "the whole rendered text as one parameter" does not work. `src/language/meta-templates.ts` has seven templates (booking reminder, booking confirmed, booking not confirmed, booking rescheduled, deposit refund approved, deposit paid twice, owner approval request) in hi, en, ml and kn. Each has the wording of ONE message from `language/templates.ts`, with the record's values (business, service, date and time, amounts, hours, number, booking ref) as numbered parameters, and starts and ends with fixed words. The agent fills `TemplateMessage.parameters` from the same facts it renders from, and uses a real template only when filling its approved wording gives back **exactly** the text the outgoing check read; otherwise it sends the generic name with no parameters, as before (a real provider then refuses it). So what the customer receives is what the check read, and the check still runs on the full rendered text before anything is sent. `whatsapp-templates.json` is the list in the form of Meta's create-template call (`node scripts/whatsapp-templates.ts` writes it; a test fails when it is out of date). The in-memory fake and the 183 tests see the same text as before; the harness approves the new names too. Messages made of several templates, and most other messages, have no template yet and fail closed outside the window (Deferred.md). **Most owner notices were in that group**, because some owner lines started with the customer's number and Meta does not accept a body that starts with a variable. That is done now: see "Joining it up", 6.

**7. Webhooks and the inbox.** `webhooks.ts` has the two signature checks (hex HMAC-SHA256 of the raw body; Meta's with the `sha256=` prefix; constant-time compare; an empty secret never verifies) and the readers: `parseRazorpayEvent` (payment_link.paid, payment.captured, refund.processed) and `parseWhatsAppWebhook` (text, a tapped button, a list row, a template quick-reply; the sender's plus put back; the timestamp turned from epoch seconds to milliseconds, which the runtime's 24-hour window depends on). The inbox is its own process because it holds the webhook secrets and the agent runtime must not. For each request it reads the raw body up to a limit (413), checks the signature on those exact bytes (401, nothing forwarded), and only then parses. It hands the events to `POST {runtimeUrl}/internal/events` with `Authorization: Bearer <internal token>` and `{"events": [...]}`, in batches under the runtime's limits (100 events, 256 KiB), and answers the provider 200 only if the runtime answered 2xx to every batch (the runtime saves before it answers). Anything else is 503 so the provider retries (Razorpay for 24 hours, Meta for 7 days); the runtime skips what it already saved by id. A webhook with nothing to forward (a status, `payment_link.expired`) is answered 200, otherwise the provider would retry it for ever. The wait on the runtime is 4 seconds because Razorpay gives up at 5. Secrets come from files (a trailing newline is dropped), never arguments, and nothing secret, no body, no number and no message text is logged.

**7b. Two ways to verify WhatsApp, chosen in the config.** The provider research recommends Gupshup for the pilot (360dialog as the fallback). Gupshup's v3 callbacks use Meta's Cloud API envelope, but its documentation shows no body signing, only an IP allowlist that has to be requested by email. So `POST /webhooks/whatsapp` has two modes, `WHATSAPP_VERIFICATION` (default `meta-signature`):
- `meta-signature`: Meta's `X-Hub-Signature-256` over the raw body, as in decision 7, with Meta's GET handshake.
- `path-token`: the callback URL carries an unguessable token, `POST /webhooks/whatsapp/<token>`, read from `WHATSAPP_PATH_TOKEN_FILE` (at least 16 characters of A-Z a-z 0-9 _ -, so it fits in a URL), compared in constant time (both sides are hashed first, so even the length is not leaked). A wrong, missing, altered or extended token is **404**, not 401, with the same body as a route that does not exist, so nothing says "right route, wrong token". The GET handshake does not exist in this mode (a GET with the right token just answers 200 "ok" for a provider that checks the URL is alive). An optional list of source addresses (`WHATSAPP_ALLOWED_SOURCES`, IPs or CIDRs, IPv4 or IPv6) refuses every other source with 403 once the token is right; behind a reverse proxy, `WHATSAPP_SOURCE_IP_HEADER` names the header the proxy sets and its last entry is the address used (a caller can put anything first). The list applies in both modes. The token is never logged, and the log no longer shows paths at all, only the route's name.
The inbox refuses to start unless one mode is fully configured, and says how. Only one mode runs at a time.

**path-token is weaker than a body signature, and that matters here.** A signature proves that THIS body came from the provider. A URL token only proves that the caller knows a secret that sits in a URL, which is logged by proxies and written in provider dashboards and email. Whoever learns the URL can post messages that look like they came from any customer or from the owner, and a message from the owner's number is what approves a held refund (plan, design rule 6). An IP allowlist narrows that but is not a substitute (it is only as good as the provider's published ranges and the proxy's header). **So with path-token, owner approvals need a per-request code**: the owner replies with a short one-use code that only the approvals relay and the owner have seen, which a forged message cannot know. That is built now (see "Approvals on WhatsApp", rule 4). Owner commands that use the booking reference (`approve b-3`) still work on the number alone, and they only ever decide small money (a late cancellation within the limit); with path-token, treat them accordingly.

**8. Stand-ins.** `standins/` is a thin HTTP layer in front of the EXISTING `createFakePayments`, `createFakeCalendar` and `createFakeMessaging`. It has no state of its own, so the tests of the fakes keep their meaning, and the contract tests read the fakes behind it as the witnesses. A fault planned as "lost reply" makes the server cut the connection after the fake stored the change, so the live adapter sees a `TimeoutError`; the other faults become the provider's own error envelope and status. The stand-ins are as awkward as the real services where it matters (see section 3). A WhatsApp template is accepted only if it is one of the approved ones, in the language asked for, with the right number of parameters, and the approved wording filled in is what lands in the outbox. Control routes under `/_standin/` let a customer write or pay (the stand-in then delivers a signed webhook to the inbox, once or twice), plan a fault, and read the outbox, events, links and calendar. They listen on 127.0.0.1 only. One difference from the fake: the live adapter cannot tell `conflicts()` from `list()` (both are `events.list`), so a fault planned on the fake's `conflicts` operation is not reachable over HTTP; plan it on `list`.

**9. Dev forwarder (Phase 0 only).** `standins/dev-forwarder.ts` reads keys from a local `.env`, adds the credential and passes each call to the real host, by path prefix (`/razorpay`, `/google`, `/whatsapp`). It prints a banner ("Dev forwarder, not Guard: no limits, no record. Test mode only."), listens on 127.0.0.1, replaces any Authorization header a caller sends, never prints a key, and refuses to start if the Razorpay key does not start with `rzp_test_` or if any `.env` value holds an `rzp_live_` key. For Google it takes a ready-made access token (about an hour). `.env.example` lists the names, with no values.

Like the rest of the folder, the new code was checked once with TypeScript 5.9 borrowed read-only from another project (`strict`, `erasableSyntaxOnly`, `verbatimModuleSyntax`, `noUnusedLocals`) with no errors. ## The monthly report and the certificates (added 3 November 2026)

Implementation plan B12, done-when items 5 and 6. A shop gets one WhatsApp message it can read in ten seconds, and a printable page and signed data behind it; before it goes live it gets a go-live certificate, and each month a monthly record certificate. All of it is in `report/`, takes plain arrays (the whole action record and the conversation store's bookings, so the runtime server can read them out of SQLite), and makes no network call.

### Three layers, one set of counts

| Layer | What | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Owner (mom test) | Five lines of "label: number" in hi, en, ml or kn (or hi and en as two messages), money and bookings first, the not-checked line last | `report/text.ts` |
| Builder | One printable page: the same numbers, a table per limit, messages by language, what is open, the lists, and "Show what we did ▸" with the counts by action and outcome, each entry's fingerprint and the digests | `report/html.ts` |
| Under the hood | The canonical JSON of the report data, sealed (digest, short code, signature) | `modules/report/sign.ts`, `report-2026-11.json` |

All three are made from one `MonthlyReport` object (`report/data.ts`). The owner text and the page only format it; they count nothing themselves. The lines are "label: number" like the product mock-up, not sentences, so no number needs a case ending in Malayalam or Kannada, and "both" is two messages of five lines each, not one long one.

### Decisions

**1. The record had to start keeping what the report counts.** An entry's `inputs` are kept only as a digest, so a deposit's amount, a refund's amount, the owner's command (approve, deny or retry) and whether a message went to the owner were not in the record at all. Two shortcuts were refused: parsing the reason strings (a rewording would silently change a count), and taking amounts from the `Booking` snapshot (a refund of a second payment is not on the booking). Instead `agent.ts` now writes four plain strings into an entry's `refs` (the entry's shape is unchanged; about 40 lines changed in `agent.ts`, 23 added and 21 removed, all of them `log(...)` calls): `amountPaise` on `record_deposit` and on `refund` (attempted, confirmed, failed and held for the owner, with `paymentId`), `command` on `owner_decision`, and `to` ("customer" or "owner") on `send_message`. An entry from before this change is still counted, but it adds nothing to a ₹ total, and `report.missing` says how many were short, so a total is never quietly low. A second, related fix: a message to the owner was recorded in the customer's language; `say()` now records the language it rendered the message in (the owner's), so "messages by language" can be counted from the record. Tests: `test/record-decisions.test.ts`.

**2. Counting rules** (each pinned by a test in `test/report-data.test.ts`, on small records and on the scripted month):
- The whole record goes in and the month is cut inside: `[from, to)` in the shop's time zone, compared with each entry's UTC time. 00:00 on 1 November in India is 18:30 UTC the evening before, and the last millisecond of 30 November belongs to November.
- Things are counted when the action happened, not by appointment date, and each thing once. A booking is "made" by its first confirmed `confirm_booking` (a retry that confirms again does not make a second booking, and a booking confirmed in October is not November's). A booking is "cancelled" only if it was confirmed before; a draft or an unpaid hold that is cancelled is counted apart (`droppedBeforeBooked`). A moved booking is counted once however often it moved. A deposit is counted by its payment id, a refund by its refund id.
- A limit's "no" is counted at one entry per rule, because a block is written twice: hours and double-booking on the `hold_slot` or `reschedule_booking` that was stopped *and* as the reason shown on the message that follows; a second payment on the blocked `record_deposit` *and* on the refund that follows. So price counts at `send_message`, hours and double-booking at `hold_slot`/`reschedule_booking`, a second payment at `record_deposit`, and the rest at `send_message`. A refund the owner is asked about is "asked you first", not "stopped". "Things stopped" counts entries, not rules: a reminder after STOP is held by two rules at once and is one thing.
- "Unconfirmed or failed" counts only those two outcomes, never `attempted`. The owner's line ("Could not confirm") counts only the open ones that touch a booking, a payment or the calendar; a message whose reply was lost is on the page, not in that number, because an owner would read the number as bookings. A later confirmed entry for the same booking (and, for refunds, the same payment) marks it "settled by a later step" instead of hiding it. A lost message is never marked settled by the next message.
- "Reminders not sent after a cancellation" from the brief's example does not happen: a cancellation clears the booking's reminders, so none is ever attempted. The reminders that are stopped are the ones after STOP.
- What the record cannot tell is listed, always, as "not counted": who turned up, whether messages were read, anything outside the agent, and whether anything got past a limit (the report counts what the limits stopped, not what slipped by). The checks' own list (the hand check's NOT TESTED) is listed beside it.

**3. Where the numbers came from is required.** `source` is `stand-in`, `test-mode` or `live`, with no default (a script that forgets it stops and asks). It is the second half of the owner text's first line ("stand-in services, test money"), a tag in the page's header, a sentence on the page, and a field in the data. A live report carries no tag.

**4. No phone numbers anywhere.** Entries are listed by booking id. The fingerprint of an entry is the sha256 of the entry *without* its `conversation` (the customer's number), so no number, not even a hash of one, reaches a page or a file.

**5. The go-live certificate does not claim more than was run.** It is made from one run of the hand check, so it carries the hand check's counts, its list of what was not tested (the list moved to `crash-test/not-tested.ts`, by id, so the hand check and the certificate cannot drift apart; the hand check prints exactly what it printed before) and its own phrase, "stand-in services". *Updated the same day, see "The hand check on a shop's own settings" below:* `scripts/go-live-certificate.ts` now runs the situations on the shop's own settings, so a shop whose situations all ran gets no caveat at all. The caveat survives in two places that are still true: a certificate built from a run on Glow Salon's settings for a different shop still adds "these checks on this shop's own settings (they ran on the example shop, Glow Salon)" (15, not 14), and a run on the shop's own settings that could not run some situations adds "some of these checks, which this shop's settings cannot run (so they were left out)" (also 15) and names them in the JSON. *Closed the same day, see "The monthly report reads the go-live certificate" below.*

**6. Banned words are in the code.** `BANNED` in `report/strings.ts`: English words checked in every language (safe, guarantee, certified, 100%, secure, proven, earn, income, profit, revenue) and each language's own (Hindi सुरक्षित, गारंटी, प्रमाणित, कमाई; Malayalam സുരക്ഷിത, ഗ്യാരണ്ടി, സർട്ടിഫൈഡ്, വരുമാനം; Kannada ಸುರಕ್ಷಿತ, ಗ್ಯಾರಂಟಿ, ಪ್ರಮಾಣೀಕೃತ, ಆದಾಯ). The words are exact: "certificate" is allowed and "certified" is not, and the confirm verbs the packs already use (पक्का, ഉറപ്പിക്കാൻ, ಖಚಿತಪಡಿಸಲು) are allowed, because "confirmed bookings" has to be sayable. A test runs the owner text, the page text, the certificate text and every string through the list, in every language and in "both".

**7. Machine-written words, listed for review.** The Hindi, Malayalam and Kannada strings in `report/strings.ts` were written by a model. Section 6 of REVIEW-ml.md and REVIEW-kn.md (made by `scripts/review-sheet.ts`, nothing above it changed, no reviewer cell was filled yet) lists every one with the English beside it and a sample of the five lines; a test fails if the sheet falls behind. Where an English technical term is kept on purpose (STOP, WhatsApp, Razorpay, webhook signatures, stand-in services) it is in Latin letters beside the translation.

### Signing and checking

The signed bytes are `"geo-document/1\n"` followed by the canonical JSON of the document (keys sorted, no spaces, UTF-8, `canonicalJson` from `record.ts`, now exported). The digest is `sha256:` and the hex of the sha256 of the canonical JSON alone. The short code ("7K2M-9QXD") is the first 40 bits of the digest in Crockford base32, re-derived from the digest every time it is checked; it is for finding a document at `/verify/<code>`, never for trusting one. The file is the canonical JSON of the whole envelope (`format`, `document`, `digest`, `code`, `signature`) and one newline, byte for byte: a pretty-printed or re-ordered or re-escaped copy of the same data is refused, so whitespace edits cannot pass. A test flips two bits of every byte of a signed file (over 4,000 edits) and deletes, inserts and truncates; every one is rejected.

What `scripts/verify-report.ts` says, and why it is careful: a signature shows that whoever held the key signed exactly these bytes and that they have not changed. It does **not** show the numbers are true (that needs the record and the services' own records), and a public key found inside the file proves nothing about whose it is: anyone can change a document and sign it again with their own key (a test does exactly that and it passes as "unchanged"). So without `--trust-key` the script says only "unchanged since key X signed it, which does not show who holds that key", and it always lists what it did not check. With `--trust-key` (a public key file, 64 hex digits or a `key:` id) it can say "signed by a key you trust". Exit codes: 0 unchanged (and trusted, if a key was named), 1 changed or not a key you named, 3 unchanged but unsigned, 64 bad use.

The key: `scripts/report-key.ts <folder>` makes an Ed25519 pair, refuses any folder inside a git work tree (a worktree's `.git` is a file; paths are resolved through links and a folder that does not exist yet), writes the private key with mode 0600, and refuses to overwrite. `--key` reads a private key from the path the caller gives and refuses a file other people can read, as ssh does. Nothing generates a key into the repository; the sample script signs with a throwaway key made in memory and never written.

**The Guard anchor.** Every document has `anchor: null`, inside the signed bytes. When Guard's hash-chained ledger exists, the issuer fills it before signing with the ledger's head (hash and length) at the moment of the report, and the same head goes into an anchor file in geometer-verify's format. Then `geo-verify` can say whether the entries the report was counted from are the ones the ledger holds, which is the one thing a signature alone cannot. The record digest in the report (the sha256 of the list of each entry's fingerprint) is what the ledger would commit to.

**This is not geometer-verify's format yet.** The conventions are the same (a domain prefix on signed bytes, `sha256:` digests, `key:` ids made from the sha256 of the raw public key, hex signatures, Ed25519), but geometer-verify's canonical form writes integers as strings and its schemas are strict and generated from `contracts/schemas/core.toml`, so `geo-verify` cannot read these files and this folder does not claim it can. Joining them means adding the report and certificate records to those schemas (and the integers-as-strings rule to this side).

### The licence's wind-down and final stop (B13, 8 November 2026)

The shop's licence comes from fleet's signed lease, which the bundle's deployer checks and hands the runtime as `client/licence.json`; the runtime reads it by its
modified time and works the state out with its own clock (`modules/licence`). **This is the runtime's own check: Guard does not read the lease yet.**

- **Wind-down: no new work.** `onBook` answers "Online booking is paused for now. Please contact <shop> directly." (`booking_paused`) before any draft is made, so
  nothing is a promise to book later; a booking waiting for "yes" is cleared the same way in `proceedToHold`, which makes no hold and no link; a greeting or an unclear
  message gets the same line instead of the menu. Each refusal is a `new_work` entry, `blocked`, with the decision `subscription_suspended`. Everything that finishes
  money owed goes on: payment events and their reconciling (a deposit paid on a link sent before the wind-down still books), cancellations and their refunds, Guard's
  held refunds, reschedules of existing bookings, reminders of existing bookings, recovery steps and the owner's commands.
- **Final stop.** On the clock's beat the open deposit links are closed at Razorpay through Guard, once for each `paid_through` (`cancel_deposit_link` with
  `refs.licence`; a UPI shop has none to close). Then a gate in `say` holds back every message, and the record says each one. Payments already made are still recorded.
- **Back in order**: new bookings are taken at once; nothing from the wind-down is done by itself (the customer asks again). One `licence` record line marks each change.
- **The owner's notice** (`server/licence.ts`, sent by `modules/licence/notice.ts` from the runtime core): `owner_licence_paused` at the wind-down,
  `owner_licence_stopped` at the final stop (bookings still to come, other to-do items, and until when the partner can give the free copy), once for each
  `paid_through`, as the utility templates `owner_service_paused` and `owner_service_stopped` when the owner's window is closed. They name the partner and never
  mention money. Hindi, Malayalam and Kannada are machine-written and wait for a native speaker's review (REVIEW-ml.md, REVIEW-kn.md).

## Joining it up (added 3 November 2026)

The runtime server, the live adapters, the inbox and the stand-ins were built on separate branches, each tested against a stand-in for the others (a recorder for the runtime, a scripted module for the adapters). This section is what happened when they were run together.

**1. The whole chain, as real servers.** `scripts/local-stack.ts` (`startLocalStack`) starts the stand-ins, the inbox (Meta signature mode) and a runtime in live mode in one process, each on a free port. The secrets are generated into files in a temp folder and read from there by the inbox and the runtime, as in production. The client folder is a copy in the same temp folder, with `runtime.json` rewritten for live mode and the live adapters' base URLs pointing at the stand-ins (Guard will sit there; nothing else differs). `test/full-chain.test.ts` runs three scenarios on it and judges each from the stand-ins' own records (calendar, payments, outbox), never from what the agent said: a Hindi booking and payment, with the provider's repeat changing nothing; the runtime stopped after the link and before the payment, so the inbox answers 503, and a new runtime on the same data folder and port, handed the events again, completes the booking once (the first message sent again is a no-op too); and a forged payment and a forged message (bad signature, none) turned away at the inbox. The three take about 0.3 seconds together.

**2. What it found: the runtime refused a real `payment.captured`.** `server/events.ts` wanted a link id in every payment event except refunds, but a real `payment.captured` names the payment and not its link (Explanation, Live services, 3). The runtime answered 400, so the inbox answered the provider 503 for that webhook, for ever; only the `payment_link.paid` that follows got through, so a booking was confirmed only if that second event arrived. Each side's tests passed because the recorder in `e2e.test.ts` accepted anything. The check now takes a payment id for `payment.captured`; a payment event with no id at all is still refused.

**3. The seams.** One: the stand-in's `POST /_standin/razorpay/deliver` takes `{"all": true}` and sends every event so far again, which is what a provider does with an event the receiver did not accept, or with a late repeat. Without it, an event counts as delivered once the stand-in has tried it, even when the inbox said 503, so the provider's retry could not be played. Nothing changed in the runtime: base URLs come from `runtime.json`, the clock from `startClient`'s option, and the stack picks the runtime's port in advance and keeps it across a stop and start (the inbox forwards to a fixed address).

**4. One command for it.** `npm run stack` is the same `startLocalStack` behind a command line: defaults to the ports in the README (8790, 8788, 8780; `--free-ports` lets the system choose), copies the example client (or `--client <folder>`, with its old `data` left behind) so nothing is written inside the repository, prints the three `curl`s, and on Ctrl+C or SIGTERM stops the three servers and removes the temp folder. The "try it" message names tomorrow, or the day after when the shop is closed tomorrow, so it works on a Saturday too. `test/full-chain.test.ts` runs the command as a child process, plays the printed `curl`s as they are printed, and checks that SIGTERM exits 0 and the folder with the secrets is gone.

**5. The model parser as a client setting.** `runtime.json` takes an optional `parser` (`wire`, `baseUrl`, `model`, `timeoutMs`), and `startClient` builds `createModelParser` from it; left out, the rule parser reads the messages as before (`StartOptions.parser` still overrides both, for tests). The config check refuses, in plain words and without repeating what was written, a parser that is not an object, an unknown wire, a base URL that is not an address or that carries a user name, a password or a query or fragment (where a key hides), a missing model, a bad timeout, and any field named like a key (`apiKey`, `token`, `secret`, `Authorization` ...). No key goes anywhere in `runtime.json`: the base URL is the Guard in front of the model provider, and Guard adds the key, as the parser already assumes. `server/main.ts` says which reader is on (the model's origin and wire, never the path). `test/start.test.ts` runs a client with a stub model server: with no `parser` the stub is never asked and a message the rules cannot read gets "I didn't understand"; with one, the stub gets one call at the configured address with no `Authorization` header, and its reading of the same message is what the agent acts on.

**6. Owner notices outside the owner's 24-hour window.** The owner usually has not written to the number today, so a plain message cannot reach them; only the approval request had a template, and the other notices (the slot or the link failed, the calendar would not confirm a paid booking, a late cancellation, a double payment ...) were refused by the provider and recorded as failed. The customer was told "I've told the owner", and the owner was not. `test/standins.test.ts` used to document that; it now asserts the opposite. Every notice the agent sends the owner on its own has a template, nine in all with the approval request (`owner_unknown_command`, the reply to the owner's own message, needs none: they have just written). The agent needed no change to use them: it already sent whatever template matches the text the check read; two pairs of owner ids were joined (below).
- *Which lines had to change.* A body may not start or end with a value. Ten Hindi and three English owner lines started with the customer's number; the Malayalam and Kannada ones did not (they begin "ഉപഭോക്താവ്" and "ಗ್ರಾಹಕರು", "customer"; the earlier note in Deferred.md said they did). English now starts "Customer {number} ...", Hindi "ग्राहक {number} ...". Nothing else was reworded in Malayalam and Kannada except the two joined notices. The in-window text and the template are the same words by construction: the template's body is filled with the record's values and must give back exactly the text the check read, or the generic name is used (and a test fails).
- *Parameters are record values only:* the customer's number, service, date and time, deposit and refund amounts, hours of notice, booking reference. Never a phrase for what happened.
- *Fewer templates than notices, where that is honest.* The slot could not be held, and the link could not be made, are one notice (`owner_not_booked`: "the calendar or the payment link did not work. Nothing is booked.") because the owner does the same either way and the customer has been told to try again; which of the two failed stays in the record. A payment that arrived after the hold ended, and a payment for a booking that was cancelled, are one notice (`owner_payment_unused`: "that booking was no longer active (its hold had ended, or it was cancelled). ₹200 was refunded."), because the money is back and there is nothing to do. Notices that ask for a decision (refund approval, late cancellation, the calendar would not confirm) and a double payment (where the booking stands) each keep their own, since one sentence is not true of both. That is nine templates for eleven situations: 36 submissions in four languages, 60 in all with the six customer ones (28 before).
- *Why not one generic owner template.* A body like "Geometer needs your attention on booking {ref} for {number}. Reply "show {ref}" to read it." would be four submissions, and it is allowed under Meta's rules as long as the variables are record values. It was rejected for three reasons. It must be the in-window text too, because the agent insists the template filled in equals the text the check read, so the owner would lose the detail inside the window as well, or the two would differ and the check would no longer cover what the owner receives. The owner would learn that something needs attention, not what, how much money, or what to reply: the approve, deny and retry commands could not travel in it unless the real text waited in a queue until the owner replied, which is new saved state, a new way to fail (the owner never replies, and a held refund waits) and more code. And putting what happened into a variable ("{{1}}") is exactly the body Meta rejects as a variable standing for the message, and would break the rule that parameters are record values. The cost of the choice is 32 more strings for a native speaker to read in each of Hindi, Malayalam and Kannada, and 32 more templates that Meta may reword or refuse.
- *Tests.* `test/owner-notices.test.ts` plays nine situations with an owner whose window is closed and checks the template, its filled wording, and that no send was recorded as failed; and that every owner id but the reply has a template in all four languages that the outgoing check passes. `test/meta-templates.test.ts` (unchanged) already checks every body for Meta's start-and-end rule and numbering, and that `whatsapp-templates.json` is current.

**Choices to review (joining it up).**
1. The two joined pairs of owner notices (item 6): which service failed, and whether the hold had ended or the booking was cancelled, are in the record and no longer in the owner's WhatsApp.
2. The stand-in's `{"all": true}` resends every payment event, accepted or not, so the full-chain restart test does not depend on which ones failed. A stand-in that remembered the failures would be closer to a provider's retry and is not built.
3. `payment.captured` is now accepted with a payment id alone. It is safe because the agent acts on a payment only through what the payments service says about it, never on the body of an event (decision 2, 'Payment events say "look"').

**Tests.** 362 before this section, 381 after (19 new: the full chain 4, the runtime's event check 1, the parser setting 3 in config and 1 in start, the owner notices 10 with the converted stand-in test counted in the old total). The hand check still says "Held 33 of 33".

## Approvals on WhatsApp (added 3 November 2026)

A refund above the shop's limit is asked of the owner **once**, by a process that is not the agent, and the agent cannot approve anything. Built against the real `geo-guard` (one integration test runs the binary), a stub Guard speaking its documented shapes, and the stand-ins. Not run against a real Razorpay or WhatsApp account.

```text
 agent ── POST …/refund {"amount":20000,"receipt":"b-1:pay_X"} ──► Guard ── above the threshold ──► 202 held, approval id, expiry
   │ keeps: approval id + the EXACT bytes                                                   (Guard-Held, guard.detail.approval)
   │
 approvals relay ◄── reads Guard's held refunds (an approver's reading credential) ── payment + amount in paise come from here
   │   asks the runtime (read-only) who/which service/which slot, checks it against Guard ── disagree: no ask, builder told
   ├─► owner (WhatsApp, owner's language, the template's own template): … Reply "approve K7M2QX" or "deny K7M2QX" within 24 hours.
 inbox ── the owner's reply goes to the relay AND (as before) to the runtime ──► relay: owner's number? live code? first use?
   ├─► Guard POST /admin/notify (HMAC-SHA256 of the raw body with the owner's secret, at_ms, once-only nonce)
   ├─► runtime: {kind:"approval_decision", approval, decision}  ──► agent sends the identical bytes again ──► one refund
   └─► owner: "Thank you. The ₹200 refund for +91… is being sent, and they will be told."
```

**Files.** `modules/core/http.ts` (`classify`) and `faults.ts` (the `Guard…Error` kinds); `src/guard-limits.ts` (settings → the limits spec); `modules/payments/guard-model.ts` (Guard's refund behaviour, from the spec); `modules/approvals/code.ts`; `src/agent.ts` (the refund flow, `approval_decision`); `server/` (the event check, `GET /internal/refund-context`, refund counts in `/status`, `services.*.agentTokenFile`); `inbox/` (the second target); `approvals/` (`main.ts`, `relay.ts`, `clients.ts`, `store.ts`, `server.ts`); `modules/standins/guard.ts` (the stub Guard over HTTP); `scripts/guard-spec.ts`; tests `guard-responses`, `guard-limits`, `refund-held`, `approvals-runtime`, `approvals-relay`, `approvals-guard-binary`, and one more in `full-chain`.

### What Guard answers, and how the adapters read it (part A)

Every response is classified in ONE place (`http.ts`, `classify`) before an adapter reads a body, using the fixtures in `test/fixtures/guard-responses.json`, copied from `monorepo/docs/guard-for-pathways.md` (several re-captured from the real binary). A body with a `guard` object is Guard's decision and is **never** the service's reply, whatever its status: a held call is HTTP **202**, a 2xx that a "2xx means done" reader would call a paid refund. It becomes a `GuardHeldError` (approval id or `null`, expiry, and the exact bytes sent), `GuardRefusedError` (`kind: "limit"` or `"rule"`, told apart by the words of `reason`, the only thing Guard gives), `GuardUnavailableError` (`guard_unavailable`, `busy` with its Retry-After, `provider_unreachable`: nothing was sent), `GuardOutcomeUnknownError` (504, `Guard-Outcome: unknown`), `GuardUnauthenticatedError` or `GuardConflictError`. All extend `ApiError`, so code that already treats an `ApiError` as "did not work" is right without change. A 200 with `Guard-Replayed: true` is the service's first reply, marked `replayed`. Anything else is the service's own answer: a Razorpay 5xx has no `guard` object, and for a refund it **may have happened**. "Could not connect" is now a `NotSentError` (an `ApiError`, 503, nothing was sent), apart from a provider 5xx; a timeout stays a `TimeoutError` (it may have happened). One adapter fix: a payment record Guard refuses to show used to be read as "no refunds yet" (`.catch(() => null)`); a Guard error is now raised.

### One threshold (part B1)

`src/guard-limits.ts` turns the settings into the Razorpay limits spec (schema 1, `monorepo/docs/guard-limits-spec.md`): `refund-needs-owner` has `threshold: String(refundLimitInr * 100)`. Both sides compare with "above" (equal passes). `test/guard-limits.test.ts` checks, for ten limits and the amounts at, under and over each, that the agent's `checkRefundLimit` and the generated threshold hold exactly the same refunds, and that ₹200 is `"20000"` and not `"200"` or `"2000000"` (a hundredfold slip is a silent fail-open or an owner approving everything). The deposit bound is the settings' deposit exactly (a link for any other amount is refused by Guard, so a deposit outside the bound would refuse every link), and a daily refund cap smaller than one deposit is refused when the spec is made (an approved refund could still be refused). `scripts/guard-spec.ts` prints it for a client folder. The same spec feeds `guard-model.ts`, so the harness, the hand check, the demo and the stub Guard all hold the refunds the real Guard will. If a refund above the limit nevertheless goes out unheld, the agent records it and raises an `unheld` issue for the builder ("Guard's threshold does not match these settings"): the only way to see a fail-open from this side.

### Above the limit, Guard asks (part B)

| The refund | Who asks the owner |
| --- | --- |
| within the limit, in time | nobody; Guard lets it through |
| above the limit (any time) | the relay, once. The agent sends the refund, Guard holds it, the agent records `held_for_owner` with the approval id and keeps the exact body; the customer is told the owner will confirm (or, for a late cancellation, will decide) |
| within the limit, cancelled late | the agent, as before: "approve b-3" / "deny b-3" (a business question) |
| a duplicate payment or an unbookable deposit, above the limit | the relay, once |

A late cancellation **above** the limit is not asked by the agent first, because the agent would ask, Guard would hold, and the relay would ask again. The calendar case ("could not confirm; reply approve to cancel and refund") is a different question from the money: if its deposit is above the limit the owner can still get a second message, for the refund (Deferred).

**No blind retries.** The agent never sends a held refund again on a timer. It sends it again only when the relay's `approval_decision` says approve, with the stored bytes, byte for byte (the test compares the held body with the resent one; `60000` and `"60000"` are different calls to Guard). It stops on deny: the payment goes in `stoppedRefunds`, because Guard would hold the same call again under a new approval and the relay would ask again. A decision for an approval the agent does not hold does nothing and is in the record. The record says what is known: the relay's word is logged as an `attempted` owner decision, and "the owner approved" (`confirmed`, which the monthly report counts) is written only when Guard has released the held call, so a forged event cannot raise the count. A forged `approve` (no approval in Guard) makes the agent ask Guard once more, and Guard answers with the **same** hold, so nothing new is asked. What a clock does retry: a refund **that was never sent** (Guard unavailable or busy, the service's own 4xx), after 1, 2, 5, 15, 30 and 60 minutes and then the builder is told; and an **approved** refund Guard could not be reached for, on the same schedule. A hold nobody answers is not retried; at Guard's expiry the agent drops it, tells the customer plainly (`refund_expired`), and raises an `expired` issue.

**Look before every send.** Before a refund and before sending a held one again, the agent reads `GET /v1/payments/{id}/refunds` and treats a refund with its receipt as done (it then says it was already there and does not send). If it cannot look, it does not send. After a 2xx it reads back **once** and never sends again whatever the list shows (the real Guard turns a repeat after a success into a new hold, which the relay would ask the owner about: see the answers below).

**`outcome_unknown` is never repeated.** Not by Guard's code, not by a provider 5xx (no `guard` object, and Guard records that as unknown too), not after the owner approved, not by any answer that arrives later. One look at the refunds list may show it; if not, the record has an `unconfirmed` entry saying "NOT sent again, check Razorpay for payment …, tell Guard (`POST /admin/intents/resolve`)", the booking gets an `unknown` issue, `/status` counts it, the runtime logs a plain line, the customer is told it is being checked (`refund_checking`), and the owner is told nothing: they cannot settle it. The same for a hold with no approval id (`detail.approval: null`): nothing for the owner to approve, an `uncertain` issue for the builder. A refusal by a limit or a rule (`refused`) and Guard saying no for good (`failed`) are builder issues too.

### Answers from the real binary (part B6)

Run on 3 November 2026 against the production-profile Razorpay template (a stub provider), and kept as assertions in `test/approvals-guard-binary.test.ts`.

1. **Does an identical repeat while the approval is pending return the same hold?** Yes: the same approval id, even with the keys reordered or re-spaced (`-1`, `-1`, `-1`). A different type (`"60000"` for `60000`) or a different receipt is a different call and a **new** hold (`-5`, `-7`). After the approval is used and the refund sent, the identical call is held **again under a new id** (`-10`), and only once that is approved is it answered from the record; after a **deny**, the identical call is likewise held again under a new id (`-19`). So a repeat is safe to poll with, but never needed, and a repeat after a success or a no would make the relay ask the owner about money already settled: the agent does not repeat. (A relay that sees a hold the runtime is not holding says so to the builder instead of asking: `stale` / `no_booking`.)
2. **What is the longest `approval_ttl_seconds`?** 604800 (7 days). The compiler accepts 60 to 604800 and refuses 604801 and 59; a Guard run on a 604800 bundle held a call with `expires_at_ms` exactly 604800000 ms after the hold. **This template uses 86400 (24 hours)**: a salon owner will not answer inside 15 minutes, an evening cancellation is usually seen next morning, and a day is long enough that "the owner never answered" means something. The approver's reading credential can be issued for a year or more (`ttl_seconds` was accepted up to 31536000); a month is a reasonable choice, and the builder renews it before it ends (Deferred). **After expiry** the relay replies once to a late "approve/deny": "That request ran out before you answered, so nothing was refunded: ₹200 for +91…", and nothing more to later ones.

**Not run against the real binary.** The refusal of a late resend ("approval … expires in N s, before the provider could answer: ask for a fresh approval") and the answer to a signed notification for an approval that has run out ("… expired before it was decided", 409) come from `guard-for-pathways.md`, not from a run. The agent and the relay read them by the words "expire" and "expired", and the model and the stub Guard say the same; a real run needs an approval of at least 60 seconds and a wait, so it is not in the tests. The wire shapes of held, refused, down, outcome-unknown and replayed were captured from the binary on 3 November (the fixtures), and the owner's approve, the deny, the replay and the single use are tests against it.

### The eight rules, and why each is there

1. **The relay holds the owner's approver secret (`notify_secrets`), from a file; the runtime never does.** Whatever takes over the runtime (a bug, a bad dependency, a model talked into it) can ask for any refund and approve none. The relay's reading credential is an **approver** credential that Guard issues from its admin port and expires, never an admin token: an admin token can resolve an uncertain intent as "did not happen" (which allows a second refund) and can switch Guard off. The relay runs beside Guard (same network, loopback admin port), as Guard's docs say. One thing to know: Guard has no read-only approver, so the reading credential can itself call `POST /admin/approvals/<id>/approve`. Its file is as sensitive as the owner's secret, and only the relay may hold either.
2. **It finds held refunds by reading Guard, and what the owner is asked comes from Guard's held call.** The payment and the amount in paise are read from the review's typed arguments (`["int","20000"]`, `["str","pay_X"]`); an amount that came as text is a different call and is not shown. The runtime adds context only (who, which service, which slot, the owner's language) through `GET /internal/refund-context?receipt=b-1:pay_X` (internal token, read-only, 404 unless that refund is held); the agent already puts the booking id in the receipt. The relay asks only if the runtime's payment, amount **and approval id** equal Guard's. If not, it does not ask and raises an alert (`disagree`, `stale`, `no_booking`, `no_receipt`, `other_agent` for a hold made by a different agent id, `cannot_decide` if its credential is not an approver who may decide; note that this reports on the reading credential's approver, not on the owner approver that signs the answer, so a wrong owner id or secret shows only when Guard refuses the signed answer: the owner is asked to try again, and the builder gets `guard_refused_401`). The runtime is given two minutes to show a booking, since it saves after Guard holds. Why: the runtime is the part that talks to customers and a model; the owner must be asked about what Guard will release, not about what the runtime says.
3. **A fresh code for each request**, 6 characters from 31 (no `0 O 1 I L`, no hyphen so it can never be read as `b-3`), one use, valid until Guard's expiry **less 60 seconds** (Guard needs the approval to have 15 seconds left when the call is sent again, and the agent needs a moment). Only its hash is kept; the clear text lives in the relay's file only until the message is out, so a resend after a failed send carries the same code (one request, one code). The words are the template's own template, `owner_approval_request` (id `owner_refund_needs_approval`), in the owner's language: `Approval needed: refund ₹200 for +919811111111 (haircut, Thu 5 Nov, 6:00 pm). Reply "approve K7M2QX" or "deny K7M2QX" within 24 hours.` It is checked by the outgoing check like every message (the code is masked as a literal), and goes as free text inside the owner's 24-hour window and as the approved template outside it. It leaves through the relay's **own** WhatsApp Guard (`WHATSAPP_GUARD_URL`, its own credential), a principal the runtime cannot reach. Why a code and not the booking ref: the ref is known to the customer, the runtime and every log; the code is known to the relay and the owner's phone.
4. **The owner's reply reaches the relay straight from the inbox, never through the runtime.** The inbox gives the owner's messages (messages from `OWNER_WHATSAPP`) to the relay with the relay's **own** bearer token, and to the runtime as before; it answers the provider 200 only if both took them (the relay saves a message before it answers, and drops an id it has seen, so a provider's retry changes nothing). The relay accepts a decision only from the owner's number, with a live code, once: the code's use is a compare-and-set in its database. **In path-token mode the code is what stands between a leaked callback URL and a forged approval**: the sender's number is only as true as the URL is secret, so a code that only the relay and the owner's phone have seen is the second factor. Codes are also guarded against guessing: 10 replies in an hour with a code that matches nothing, from the owner's number, and every code is ignored until the hour is up, and the builder is told (a forger can use this to stop approvals for an hour; refusing to approve is the safe way to fail). A reply from another number is dropped before the code is read.
5. **On `approve <code>` the relay gives Guard the owner's signed answer; on `deny <code>`, the same with a no.** `POST /admin/notify`, body `{approval, decision, approver, at_ms, nonce}`, `X-Geo-Signature` = HMAC-SHA256 of the raw body. `at_ms` is the relay's own clock (the message's time may be older than Guard's five minutes if the provider redelivered), the nonce is new on every try. Order: Guard (the authority), then the runtime, then the owner, each remembered, so a crash is finished on restart (tested): "approve" is told to Guard once, and the runtime is told as soon as it is back. If Guard cannot be reached, or refuses (wrong secret), the code is **not** used up and the owner is asked to send it again; if Guard already has the same answer, that counts as done. The event to the runtime is `{kind:"approval_decision", approval, decision}` plus an `id` that is new on every delivery, **because the test found a hole without it**: an earlier forged `approval_decision` for the same approval had the same event key and made the runtime drop the real one as a repeat. It carries no authority: Guard releases a refund only if its own record shows the approval.
6. **The agent silently ignores `approve <code>` / `deny <code>` in owner messages** (`RELAY_CODE_REPLY`): no reply, no record. Both read the owner's messages; the agent saying "I didn't understand" about an answer the relay understood would confuse the owner. `approve b-3` for a refund Guard holds is recorded as blocked and gets no reply either: that refund is answered with the code in the ask. A customer who types the same words is an ordinary customer.
7. **A hold with `detail.approval: null` goes to the builder, never to the owner**, since it cannot be approved on WhatsApp: the agent raises the `uncertain` issue, `/status` has `refunds.needBuilder`, the runtime logs "A refund needs the builder, not the owner (…)", and the relay, which can only ever see holds that have an approval id, counts and logs any review item without one. The fleet alert for this is later (Deferred).
8. **A late reply** (the code has run out, or Guard says "expired before it was decided") is answered once, plainly: that the request ran out and nothing was refunded. The agent drops its hold at Guard's expiry and tells the customer.

### Equipment rental on the Bookings template

`settings/equipment-rental.example.json` is a made-up Karnataka village hiring centre (Hosahalli Farm Hiring Centre): one tractor, rented with its attachments (rotavator half day ₹3,200 and full day ₹5,600, sprayer half day ₹2,400, trolley full day ₹4,000), open 6 to 6 every day, bookings starting every 2 hours from midnight, an hour's gap after each, a ₹1,000 deposit held 30 minutes, a 24-hour cancellation window, a ₹1,000 refund limit, one reminder 24 hours before, Kannada first and Hindi second, the owner in Kannada. It is settings and nothing else: no template code changed for it.

Why these numbers, because each one is a limit of the settings and not a taste:

- The slot grid starts at midnight, so a 240-minute grid would give starts at 4, 8, 12, 16 and 20 and no 6 am. A 120-minute grid gives the 6, 8, 10 ... starts a farm day wants.
- A job cannot be longer than 8 hours (`durationMinutes` is at most 480), so "full day" is 8 hours, not dawn to dusk.
- One deposit for every job, and never more than the cheapest. ₹1,000 is under the sprayer's ₹2,400.
- Reminders are "hours before", not a time of day. A second reminder 2 or 12 hours before a 6 am start would arrive at night, so there is one, 24 hours before, which lands at the same time of day as the booking.
- Every name must be different, and a farmer who writes only the machine ("rotavator") gets the price list and is asked which job. Each job has its full name and a shorter way of saying it. A bare "tractor" matches nothing, on purpose.
- The jobs are all one tractor with different attachments, so the shop-wide "one booking at a time" rule is exactly right for it. A centre with a tractor and a separate pump set cannot use one file for both (below).

**What Bookings cannot do for rental** is the list in Deferred.md under "Equipment rental". I chose not to change template code: the gap that matters most (several machines at the same time) needs a capacity per service in the calendar rule, in Guard's spec, in the report and in the demo, which is not small; the 8-hour limit is a one-number change, but it is a limit the owner and the tests have documented, so it is listed with its suggested change rather than changed here.

**The variant sell guide** (`sell/variants/equipment-rental.<lang>.md`) covers sections 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 11 and points to the main guide for 2, 9 and 10. Its test checks what it says against the settings and the real hand check ("29 of 29 situations held"), drives the demo through its four taps in Hindi and Kannada, and found one thing worth keeping: the demo has one customer, so a second booking for the same customer replaces the first, and it cannot show two machines clashing. ## A voice note gets a reply (added 3 November 2026)

**Why.** A WhatsApp voice note used to vanish: `textOf` in `modules/messaging/webhooks.ts` returned nothing for any type but text, a button or a list row, so the inbox forwarded nothing and the customer heard nothing. A shop customer who sends a voice note and gets silence thinks the shop ignored them. Until a voice module exists (plan B29) the agent now answers with one plain line.

**What it says, and to whom.** "Please type your message; I can't listen to voice notes yet." (template `voice_note`, in en, hi, ml and kn; the review sheets list it). The language is the one the conversation already has, taken from the customer's earlier messages if the store knows one, otherwise the shop's first language, the same way every other reply picks it. A voice note does not change that language. It is sent as a normal text reply: the customer has just written, so the 24-hour window is open. If the send fails or its reply is lost, `say` records it as `failed` or `unconfirmed` like any other message and the step goes on. A customer who sent STOP gets nothing: the agent returns before sending, the same place a text from an opted-out customer stops. The shop owner's voice note gets the same line in the owner's language, because the owner's messages are commands and a silent drop is worse than a line.

**How it travels.** Meta sends `type: "audio"` with an `audio` object (`voice: true` for a recorded voice note, absent or false for an audio file; both are treated the same). `parseWhatsAppWebhook` turns it into an `InboundMessage` with empty `text` and `voiceNote: true`, and reads nothing else of the `audio` object: the media id, the file type and the hash are left behind on purpose, so they cannot reach the saved events, the agent or the record. `checkEvents` accepts `voiceNote` only as the boolean `true`, and only with empty text; anything else is a 400, like any malformed event. The event key is still `message:<id>`, so a redelivered voice note is a duplicate in the store (one task) and, if it ever reached the agent twice, is ignored there by the seen-message list: one reply either way. The saved task holds the flag and no words.

**What the record holds.** A `receive_message` entry (confirmed, with the note "a voice note: it is not listened to and nothing of it is kept") and a `voice_note_reply` entry for the reply, with the same outcome words as a `send_message` (`confirmed` only after the provider's own outbox shows the message). It holds the reply's template id, who it went to and OUR message id, never an audio id or link. `say` takes an optional action name for this; with it a reply is one entry, not two, so a lost reply cannot be counted twice.

**What the report does with it.** `report/data.ts` has one list of the actions that are a message going out (`MESSAGE_ACTIONS`: `send_message` and `voice_note_reply`). A confirmed reply counts as a message to a customer in its language; a lost one is listed among the messages not confirmed and does not make the owner's "Could not confirm" line, which counts bookings, payments and the calendar. A limit's "no" on such a message is counted where it is for any message. An action name the report has never heard of is counted only in the table by action on the page and nowhere else (a test adds one).

**Not done, on purpose.** The audio is not fetched, transcribed or kept. A customer who sends five voice notes gets five lines (one per message, as five unclear texts get five "I didn't understand"). The equipment-rental sell guide used to say a voice note gets no reply; it now says it gets a line asking the farmer to type.

## The monthly report reads the go-live certificate (added 3 November 2026)

**Why.** The monthly report and its certificate carried "these checks on this shop's own settings (they ran on the example shop, Glow Salon)" for every shop but Glow Salon, because the report was never told what the checks ran on: `checkedPolicyDigest()` simply defaulted to Glow Salon's. After the hand check learned to run on a shop's own settings that note was often untrue, and a shop whose checks did run on its own settings was told they had not.

**What changed.** `client-report.ts` (and `writeClientReport`, `buildMonthlyReport`) take `--go-live <go-live-certificate.json>`. `report/go-live.ts` reads it: the file goes through `verifyEnvelopeText`, the same check `verify-report.ts` makes (canonical form, digest, code, signature), and must be signed; then the document must be a go-live certificate with the fields the report uses. Its `checkedPolicyDigest` (the digest of the settings the situations ran on) is compared with `policyDigest(settings)` of this shop. That gives `report.goLive`, one of five findings, written into the signed data and copied into the monthly certificate:

| Finding | When | What the report says | Added to "not tested" |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `own` | good signature, the digest is this shop's | the go-live checks ran on this shop's own settings, in the certificate of that date; the certificate's own counts ("Held 33 of 33 situations. 0 failed. 14 not tested."); the key that signed it | nothing (or "situations-skipped" if the certificate says it left some out) |
| `example` | good signature, the digest is Glow Salon's | the certificate says its checks ran on the example shop, Glow Salon, not on this shop's settings | "own-settings" (the old wording) |
| `different` | good signature, any other digest | the certificate says its checks ran on different settings, so it does not show how this shop's own settings behave | "other-settings" |
| `untrusted` | unsigned, changed, re-spaced, signed wrongly, not a go-live certificate, unreadable | a certificate was given but could not be trusted, so it was not used | "go-live-untrusted" |
| `none` | no `--go-live` | no go-live certificate was given, so the document does not say what the checks ran on | "no-go-live-certificate" |

**Decisions.**
- **A bad certificate never stops the report.** The script prints one plain sentence ("The go-live certificate could not be trusted (it is not signed ...), so it was not used") and still writes the same six files, exit 0, and the report says it too. A missing or unreadable file is the same case, not a usage error, unlike `--settings` or `--record`.
- **Counts only for `own`.** Counts from settings that are not this shop's would be read as this shop's, so `example` and `different` carry the date and the key and no counts. Nothing says "safe": the counts are the certificate's, written by the same formatter.
- **The key is named, and it is said not to prove who holds it.** A signature shows that a key signed these bytes and nothing changed; anyone can sign a made-up certificate with their own key (a test does, and the report names the stranger's key). Like `verify-report.ts` without `--trust-key`, the report says "signed with key:..., a key does not show who holds it". A trust option for the go-live key is not built (Deferred.md).
- **No `--go-live` is not Glow Salon.** Glow Salon's own monthly report without a certificate also says none was given; its example (`report-example.ts`) makes the go-live certificate first and reads it back, so the sample shows the real case.
- **The owner's five lines do not change.** The finding adds one item to the not-tested list, which the fifth line counts in "and N more"; the sentences are on the page and in the monthly certificate's text.
- **The sentences carry no digit.** The date, the counts and the key are filled in by the formatters, in `report/strings.ts` in the four languages (machine-written for hi, ml and kn; section 6 of REVIEW-ml.md and REVIEW-kn.md lists them for a native reader).

**What the tests pin.** `test/report-go-live.test.ts`: the five cases (a good signature with the shop's digest, with Glow Salon's and with another shop's; a tampered, re-spaced, unsigned, wrong-kind, thin, junk and empty file; none given), every case in the four languages in the owner's text, the page and the monthly certificate, and the real scripts: `go-live-certificate.ts` then `client-report.ts --go-live` on the same settings file (the digest has to survive JSON), the same certificate on another shop's file, and another shop's certificate on Glow Salon's.

**A public function changed.** `buildMonthlyReport` takes an optional `goLive` and the report has a new `goLive` field, so every monthly report's digest is different from before; `NotTestedKey` has three new values; `MonthlyCertificate` has `goLive`. `notTestedFor` and `checkedPolicyDigest` are unchanged and still serve the go-live certificate itself.

## A refund is never more than one deposit (added 4 November 2026)

The owner asked: if a customer paid ₹100 and says "refund ₹200", we refund ₹100 and are not confused by what they wrote, and Guard enforces it as well.

**What already held** (checked first, not redone). The agent refunds `depositInr`, the deposit on the booking's own record; the words of a message never set the amount. A message like "please cancel today's 6 pm. Refund my ₹500?" is read as a cancel, and the ₹500 is not looked at. The late-cancellation message and the owner's ask say the deposit from the same record. The agent's own `checkRefundLimit` blocks any amount above what was paid.

**What was missing.** Guard looked at the size of a refund only to decide whether the owner must approve it. A refund below the owner's limit went through whatever its size. If the agent were ever wrong, or taken over, Guard would have sent a ₹499 refund of a ₹200 payment.

**The rule.** `refund-at-most-deposit`, in the generated spec (`src/guard-limits.ts`): an `argument_bound` on the `amount` of `refund`, from `"1"` (one paisa) to the deposit in paise. The deposit is the same settings value that bounds the payment link (`deposit-amount`), so the two cannot drift. A deposit is the right ceiling because every payment this agent can receive is exactly one deposit: the links are bound to it, and the second payment in "deposit paid twice" is one deposit too. The agent refunds a whole deposit, so the most a refund can be is one deposit.

**What the engine does with it** (read from the engine source, `geo-rules` `calls.rs` and Guard's `engine.rs`, then checked on the built binary):

1. `argument_bound` is not tied to payment links or to deposits. It applies to any operation and any field of the call (it follows a dotted name into nested objects, too), with both ends allowed. An amount written as text, such as `"20001"`, is read as the number it spells. A missing or non-number amount is "cannot decide", which refuses.
2. Guard decides each rule on a call by itself and then adds the answers up: any refusal (or "cannot decide") refuses, and only if nothing refused does any hold hold. The order of the rules does not matter. So a refund above both the owner's limit and the deposit is **refused (HTTP 403), not held**. No approval is made, so the owner is never asked about money that could not be refunded, and the refusal comes before Guard's once-only record for the payment is touched, so a later refund of the right size on the same payment is not blocked.
3. A refund of exactly the deposit passes the bound. If the owner's limit is below the deposit it is then held for the owner, as before; if not, it goes through.

**The model of Guard follows.** `modules/payments/guard-model.ts` (used by the hand check, the demo and the stub Guard) now decides the bound first and refuses, with the rule's id and Guard's own words ("amount 20001 is above the highest allowed value, 20000"), before it looks at the threshold. It reads the bound from the generated spec, not from the settings, as it does the threshold. The in-memory payments service had the refused rule's name written in; it now carries the id from the model.

**What the agent does when Guard refuses a refund** (unchanged code, now tested for this rule). `requestRefund` reads a refusal by a rule like any other: nothing was sent, the booking's entry in the record is `blocked` with the note "Guard refused the refund (a rule: refund-at-most-deposit): … Nothing was refunded.", the payment goes into `stoppedRefunds` so it is not asked again, the builder sees a `refused` issue (`refunds.needBuilder` in `/status`), and the owner is not asked. The customer has already been told "We are checking your ₹200 refund and will message you as soon as it is confirmed", not that it was sent.

**The edge case: the owner lowers the deposit.** Say the deposit goes from ₹200 to ₹150 and Guard is given the new spec while a booking paid at ₹200 is still open. The booking's record says ₹200, so its refund is refused: Guard cannot know that ₹200 was a deposit when it was paid. This is what the rule does when a deposit is lowered, and what the record then says (a test shows it with the Guard model, and another with the real binary). The way round is in Deferred.md: lower the deposit once those bookings are over, or refund them by hand in Razorpay. A true cap for each payment (a refund may not exceed what that payment captured, less what was already refunded) would not need a number from the settings, but it needs Guard to keep a running total for each payment, which is a new kind of rule in the engine. It is not built; it is in Deferred.md with the reason. Razorpay itself refuses a refund above the captured amount, and so does the stand-in, so this rule is a second, earlier check on our side and not the only one.

**The situation.** `refund-more-than-paid`, "Customer asks for more back than they paid" (the 34th for Glow Salon; no row in plan 4.4 fits it). It is built from the shop's settings like the others (`crash-test/situations.ts`, 15b), so it runs on any shop: the amount is the shop's own deposit, the claim is an amount above it that is not on the shop's price list, and the days and times come from `crash-test/shop.ts`. For Glow Salon: the customer pays the ₹200 deposit and cancels with "Refund my ₹500?", once more than 24 hours ahead and once 6 hours before the start. Judged from the stand-ins: the payments service's own list holds exactly one refund of ₹200 on the deposit payment (after the owner's "approve b-1" in the late case, where the agent has not asked the service at all before that); the payment shows ₹200 refunded and not a rupee more; the amounts the agent asked the service for, read at its door, are 20000 paise and nothing else; the customer's messages and the owner's ask name ₹200, and no message to either names ₹500. A shop with no cancellation window has no late cancellation, so only the first half runs and the situation says the other was left out; a shop with no time far enough ahead skips the situation and says why. ## Three-step understanding, buttons and lists, and button mode (B46, B47; added 5 November 2026)

Plan items B46 (how a message is understood, cheapest step first) and B47 (buttons, lists, and what the agent does when the AI is off). The design comes from [Economics and pricing](../../../../docs/product/economics-and-pricing.md) section 3 (the AI) and section 8 (credits and button mode).

### The router (B46)

`src/adapters/understanding.ts`. Each customer message goes through up to three steps and stops at the first that is sure.

| Step | What reads it | When it is sure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0, `rules` | A tapped button or list row (looked up by its id, no words read), then the rule parser | "STOP", yes, no, cancel, a greeting, "talk to the owner", a move that names a day or time, a booking that names a service, a day and a time, and anything that tries to change the rules |
| 1, `decision` | A System One decision model: Laya on the shop server, or Jev hosted (`modules/understanding/system-one.ts`) | It answers three **choice questions** with probabilities: what the customer wants, which of the shop's own services, and which of up to six free times taken from the calendar. The chosen option must reach the threshold (default 0.75) |
| 2, `model` | DeepSeek V4.1 Flash on DeepInfra (any OpenAI-compatible endpoint), through the existing `model-llm.ts` | Whatever it returns that `coerceIntent` accepts |

- **No model ever writes to a customer.** Every answer becomes an `Intent`, `coerceIntent` checks it, and templates write every reply.
- **Phone numbers and names are taken out first** (`modules/understanding/redact.ts`). Numbers by shape (any run of 8 or more digits, in four scripts), e-mail addresses and links by shape; names by what is known (a profile name, if given) and by "my name is", "मेरा नाम", "എന്റെ പേര്", "ನನ್ನ ಹೆಸರು" and a few others, one word and a capitalised surname. A name written some other way gets through: this is exact for numbers and best effort for names. The sender's own number never reaches the router, only the words.
- **Prompt caching.** The shop's system prompt for step 2 is the same text for every message and comes first (`model-prompt.ts`); nothing per-message goes into it. DeepInfra's reported cached tokens (`prompt_tokens_details.cached_tokens`) are priced at the cached rate.
- **Malayalam and Kannada skip step 1 by default** (`decision.languages` defaults to Hindi and English), because the Laya model card does not mention them (economics section 3.2). Add `"ml"`, `"kn"` to `decision.languages` once the eval shows they pass.
- **Unsure with nothing after it** (no step 2 configured, or it failed): the rules' reading stands, and the agent asks the customer, with a list.
- **Jev and DeepInfra are paid, so there is no call without a key.** The key is read from the environment variable a config names (`keyEnv`), sent only as `Authorization: Bearer` to that one address, and never logged, stored or put in an error. If the variable is not set the step is off and the start-up line names the variable, not its value. The tests use made-up keys.

### The settings (`runtime.json`)

| Key | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `ai` | `"on"` (default) or `"buttons"`. **`"buttons"` turns every model step off.** This is the key the bundle writes from fleet's heartbeat answer |
| `decision` | Step 1: `{ provider: "laya" \| "jev" \| "system-one", baseUrl, model, keyEnv?, threshold?, languages?, timeoutMs? }`. Jev needs `keyEnv`. Laya (a local address) needs none |
| `parser` | Step 2, as before, with `provider?` (for the record and the price list, default the host) and `keyEnv?` (the provider's key, from the environment). `keyEnv` and `agentTokenFile` are two ways to authenticate, never both. A key to plain `http://` on another machine is refused |
| `allowance` | `{ messages?, costInr? }`: this month's most messages read by steps 1 and 2, and their most estimated cost in rupees. Either reached puts the shop in button mode until the month ends |
| `pauseMinutes` | How long button mode lasts after a model step fails (default 5) |

**For whoever writes the switch (the bundle):** the file is `<client folder>/runtime.json`, the key is `"ai"`, the value is `"buttons"` to switch the AI off and `"on"` (or no `"ai"` key) to give it back. The bundle (B48) writes `"buttons"` for fleet's reason `overage_unfunded` and leaves the key out otherwise; the validator accepts both values, so it does not skip the key any more. The runtime looks at the file again whenever its modified time changes, on every customer message and every tick, so no restart is needed. Write it whole to a temporary file and rename it over `runtime.json`; a file caught half written leaves the last answer standing, never switches the AI off by accident. `Status.ai` (below) says what the agent is doing.

### Understanding record fields (for the cost meter)

One `receive_message` entry per customer message, in the existing record format (`refs` is a map of strings). The entry's other fields are as before. The cost meter reads them through `understandingOf` in `bundle/lib/usage.ts`; **the single source of truth for the names is its `FIELDS` constant, which I changed to match this branch** (`container: "refs"`, and a number may be a decimal string, as `refs` holds it); `bundle/test/usage.test.ts` also feeds it a record the agent really wrote. These are the exact keys:

| `refs` key | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `step` | `rules`, `decision` or `model`: the step that handled the message |
| `provider` | `rules`, `laya`, `jev`, `system-one` or the language model's `provider` from `runtime.json` (for example `deepinfra`) |
| `model` | The model id (`rule-parser` for the rules, `laya`, `deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash` ...) |
| `inputTokens`, `outputTokens` | Decimal strings. The total over **every** call made for this message, so a message the decision model was unsure of and the language model then read counts both |
| `costMicroInr` | Whole micro-rupees (millionths of a rupee), as a decimal string, from the one dated table of model prices (`modules/understanding/rates/<date>.json`, read by `rates.ts`), rounded up. `"0"` for the rules and for Laya. **Absent** when any call had no price in the table: an unknown price is never written as 0 |
| `rateDate` | `YYYY-MM-DD`, the day the handling call's price starts. Absent when unpriced. A record dated before the table's first rate is unpriced |
| `tried` | The steps asked, joined with `+`: `rules`, `rules+decision`, `rules+decision+model` |
| `failed` | Present only when a step was asked and could not answer, joined with `+`: `decision`, `model`, `decision+model`. The message was then read by the rules |
| `skipped` | Present only when a step was left out because it was paused for failing again and again: `decision`. The message was then read by the next step at once |
| `via` | Present only for a tapped message: `button` or `list` |

`step`, `tried` and `failed` are the shares of the planned 30/60/10 mix. A tapped message is `step: rules` with `via`.

The month's totals are also in the store and in the runtime's `status()`, counts only:

```
status().ai = { month: "2026-11", messages: { rules, decision, model }, costMicroInr, unpriced, paused: { decision: ISO time | null },
                allowance: { messages | null, costMicroInr | null }, mode: "ai" | "buttons", reason: "override" | "allowance" | "fault" | null }
```

`unpriced` is the number of messages this month whose cost is not in `costMicroInr`; the cost meter (`bundle/lib/usage.ts`) counts the same messages from the record and keeps them apart from the spend. `paused.decision` is the moment the decision model's pause ends, or null when it is being asked (see "A pause on a decision model that keeps failing" below). The prices in the table are US dollars per million tokens, each line with its source and the day it was read, converted at ₹87 to the dollar (the rate the runtime has always used; the economics note says ₹88, which is the owner's to settle in a new dated file); each rate carries the rupee rate of its file and its date.

### The one table of model prices (5 Nov)

There were two: the runtime's (`rates.ts`, three providers, in code) and the bundle's price lists (four other models, plus a copy in `eval/models.json`). There is one now: `modules/understanding/rates/2026-11-05.json` (data, dated, never edited after it is committed; a changed price is a new file). It holds seven lines: Laya (no per-call price), Jev ($0.042 per million), DeepInfra's DeepSeek V4.1 Flash ($0.16 in, $0.48 out, $0.004 cached; any id that names it matches), and Claude Haiku 5.5, Gemini 3.1 and 3.5 Flash-Lite and GPT-6 Luna from the providers' pages of 3 Nov; Sarvam and Groq are listed as not priced, with the reason. A model in no line is unknown. The runtime reads it (`rates.ts`); `scripts/model-eval.ts` reads it by each candidate's `provider` and `model` (`eval/models.json` has no prices); `bundle/panel/test/cost.test.ts` fails if any model price in any committed bundle price list disagrees with it; `modules/test/rates.test.ts` pins its bytes and runs it through `rates-check.ts`. A runtime config names the provider (`parser.provider`, `decision.provider`), so a model reached by its address alone is named after the host and has no line until one is written. The conversion is ₹87 to the dollar, as before, so no stored cost changed; the economics note says ₹88.

### The allowance, and button mode (B46 and B47)

The shop is in **button mode** when any of these holds, checked in this order. In button mode the agent reads with the rules only, answers with buttons and lists, and every limit holds exactly as before; Guard and the template's own checks do not change.

| Trigger | When | Told |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `override` | `runtime.json` says `"ai": "buttons"` | The owner, once per switch (switching it off and on again is a new switch). Record: `ai_mode`, `reason: override` |
| `allowance` | `decision` + `model` messages this month reach `allowance.messages`, or their cost reaches `allowance.costInr` | The owner, once a month. Record: `ai_mode`, `reason: allowance` |
| `fault` | A step was asked and could not answer (an error, a timeout, an answer it could not use) and **no other step could**, so the rules had to read the message. A decision model that fails while the language model answers is not a fault: it is `failed: decision` in that message's record, and after a few of those it is paused (below) | The owner, at most once a day. Button mode lasts `pauseMinutes`, then the next message tries the AI again. Record: `ai_mode`, `reason: fault` |

- **Told once.** Three new owner notices, in four languages, each a WhatsApp template (`owner_ai_allowance_used`, `owner_ai_switched_off`, `owner_ai_not_answering`; `whatsapp-templates.json` is rebuilt). The builder or partner has no WhatsApp number in the settings, so they are told by the record (`ai_mode`, with the reason and the month, in plain words in `notes`), by `status().ai` and by a start-up/runtime log line, all of which the heartbeat can carry. The "already told" flags are kept with the store, so a restart or a repeated step does not tell them again.
- **No customer is ever left without an answer.** A message the rules cannot read gets the list of the shop's services; a time the shop cannot give gets a list of other free times; a model that fails changes nothing about the reply except that the rules read it. A new test and three hand-check situations hold this (below).
- **A tapped message never asks a model, whatever the mode.** The router is not even called.

### A pause on a decision model that keeps failing (5 Nov)

Button mode is for when **no** step can read a message. The case between: the decision model (step 1) is down but the language model (step 2) answers. Before, every unclear message waited out the decision timeout (3 seconds) and then paid step 2, with no sign anywhere except `failed: decision` in that message's record. Now each step has its own small circuit breaker, kept in the store's `ai.steps` (`modules/understanding/ai-state.ts`; only the decision model has one, the language model has the fault pause above):

| What | Number (a placeholder, `DEFAULT_BREAKER`) |
| --- | --- |
| Failures that open it | 3, inside a window of 15 minutes (a success clears the count) |
| How long the step is left out | 10 minutes |
| After that | the next message tries the step once: a failure closes it again for 10 minutes at once (no new line in the record), an answer, even "unsure", ends the outage |

- **Only when something can take over.** The reader counts a decision failure only if the language model was asked after it **and answered** (with both down, the fault pause retries every few minutes, and those failures would otherwise add up to a "pause" that says the language model is reading), and the router skips a paused decision model only if it has a language model. With no step 2 a failing decision model is still the fault that moves the shop to buttons, exactly as before.
- **What the router does.** The reader hands `skipSteps: ["decision"]` to the parser while the pause runs. The message goes straight to step 2 (`tried: rules+model`), pays only step 2's price, and says `skipped: decision` in its record. The router itself still keeps no state.
- **What is written down, and for whom.** One `ai_mode` line when the pause begins (`mode: step_paused`, `step`, `reason: failures`, `until`, in plain words in `notes`) and one when the step answers again (`mode: step_resumed`); `status().ai.paused.decision` has the end time while it runs (and the runtime logs a line for each, for the builder). **Nobody is messaged**: no customer sees a difference, the owner has nothing to do, and a message costs what it did while the step failed. The state survives a restart (it is in the store) and a new month. A store saved before this has no `steps` and works.
- **Both models down** is the fault as before. Fees uses the same reader and gets the pause with no change to its agent.
- **Tests.** `modules/test/breaker.test.ts` (the numbers, the window, the single try, the month, an old store, the status) and `test/step-breaker.test.ts` (a Bookings agent against a decision model that is down and comes back: no wait on the fourth message, the record lines, the fault path unchanged).

### Buttons and lists (B47)

`src/language/interactive.ts` holds the words (four languages, machine-written like the rest, on both REVIEW sheets in the section "The words on the buttons and the lists"), the limits (Meta: 3 reply buttons of up to 20 characters; a list of up to 10 rows, titles up to 24 characters, descriptions up to 72; a body up to 1,024), and the ids. A tap is read by looking the id up: `yes`, `no`, `cancel` (asks "are you sure" first), `cancel_yes`, `keep`, `owner`, `menu`, `svc:<service id>`, `slot:<start in ms>`. A forged or stale id is read as ordinary words; a `slot:` id that is taken or outside the hours is refused like a typed time.

| Turn | What the customer gets |
| --- | --- |
| Hello, or something not understood, or "no booking found" | The list of the shop's services with prices, and a last row "Talk to the owner" (a shop with more than 9 services shows nine; the rest are reached by typing the name) |
| Service known, no time | A list of up to nine free times: the next week, at most three a day spread through it, on the shop's slot grid, inside its hours, clear of the calendar and of every hold, and at least the hold's length from now |
| A time that cannot be had | The reason, and a list of other free times |
| A read-back that needs a yes | The read-back with **Yes** and **No** buttons |
| A quote, "already waiting", "Booked!", "moved" | **Cancel booking** and **Talk to the owner** |
| Cancel booking | "Do you want to cancel your booking for ...?" with **Yes, cancel** and **No, keep it**. Nothing is cancelled by the first tap |
| Talk to the owner | The owner is told (a new owner template, at most once an hour for the same customer) and so is the customer; if the owner cannot be told, the customer is told that and not that the owner will write |

- **Attached to the message already being sent**, not an extra one. The reply is one interactive message in place of one text message, so a booking takes no more messages and the customer taps instead of typing.
- **The checked text is the whole message.** `say()` builds the body from templates and each row from the record, then the outgoing check reads the body plus every row, and `MessageFacts.slotChoices` lets the check accept exactly the offered times. The plain words are checked too, because they may be sent instead.
- **Where buttons cannot go, the words go as plain text, as before:** the customer's 24-hour window is closed (a template goes, with no buttons), the provider has no `sendInteractive`, the message breaks Meta's limits, or the provider refuses it with a 4xx. A lost reply or a 5xx may have gone out, so it is not sent again.
- **On the wire.** `modules/messaging/whatsapp.ts` sends Meta Cloud API interactive payloads (`type: "interactive"`, `button` or `list`); `modules/standins/whatsapp.ts` accepts them, refuses a body Meta would refuse (131009) and keeps them in the fake's outbox (`kind: "interactive"`, `interactive`, `text`); `webhooks.ts` keeps the tapped id and kind; `server/events.ts` passes `reply: { id, kind }` on and checks its shape. `world.tap(id)` in the harness taps an option that was actually offered.

### The System One client and stand-in

`modules/understanding/system-one.ts` is a dependency-free `fetch` client for `POST {base}/v1/systemone` (or `{base}/systemone` when the base ends in `/v1`), written from pydantic-ai's `system_one.py`: `instructions` on every question, and an answer is refused unless its names are the questions', its choice is one of the options, its probabilities are for exactly those options, each is between 0 and 1 and they add up to 1 within `1e-6 + 0.005 x options`. `modules/standins/system-one.ts` answers the same shape and enforces the same checks on the request; a test gives it a decider to make the model sure, unsure, wrong, slow or down. `systemOneFetch(standin)` runs it inside the process with no port (the hand check does this). `node standins/main.ts` does not serve it yet (Deferred.md).

## The monthly report on WhatsApp, and the Innovator badge (B54, B56; added 5 November 2026)

On the first morning of the month the owner gets last month's report on WhatsApp, from the shop's own record, in the owner's language, with the name of the builder or partner who set the agent up, and the Innovator badge when that person has it. It goes once, even if the process restarts. Everything below is in `report/` (`whatsapp.ts`, `monthly-job.ts`) except the wiring in `server/`.

### The message

The owner's lines are the B12 lines, as a fixed form, so they can also be a WhatsApp template (a template has no optional lines). Eight lines, nine with the badge:

```text
Monthly report: Glow Salon · Feb 2027
Bookings made: 38 · Deposits collected: ₹17,600
Stopped by your limits: 5 (Prices below your list: 3 · Double bookings: 1)
You were asked: 3 · You approved: 2 · You said no: 1
Could not confirm: 1
Not checked: phone calls · walk-ins · and 17 more (see the full report). Checks were run by hand.
Code: 7K2M-9QXD · Full report: https://reports.example.com/r/3f9a…/report-2027-02-en.html
Set up by: Divya, local partner · Geometer
★ Innovator
```

How each of the website's five items is made: bookings and deposits are `bookings.made` and `money.deposits`; discount requests refused and double bookings are `stopped.byRule` for the price-list rule and the double-booking rule, written as "stopped" with the total beside them (a limit stopped something; it did not "find 0 double bookings"); refunds approved is `owner.approved`, with how many were asked and refused. "Things fixed" is not on the list: it is a builder's note ("1 thing we fixed, and how"), not something the record can count. The message says how many bookings are still unconfirmed instead, and the page lists what a later step settled. The "not checked" line and "checks were run by hand" stay, as on every other document. A report made in test mode or on stand-ins says so in the first line.

The words are the ones the five-line owner text already uses (`report/strings.ts`), so they are reviewed once. The new words are "Full report" and the badge word (`fullReport`, `innovator`), in four languages. The values are numbers and amounts from the report, never words, so none takes an ending in Malayalam or Kannada. Every value goes through `oneLine`, because Meta refuses a line break, a tab or a run of spaces inside one.

### One wording, two ways to send it

Inside the owner's 24-hour window the text goes as plain text. Outside it (the usual case on the 1st) WhatsApp carries only an approved template, so the same words are two utility templates: `monthly_report` and `monthly_report_innovator` (the badge is a template of its own, because a template cannot leave a line out), each in hi, en, ml and kn: 8 entries in `whatsapp-templates.json`, with examples from the review sheets' sample month. They have fourteen values (the title, nine counts and amounts, the not-checked list, the code, the address, the name), start and end with fixed words, never put two values side by side, and have a lot of fixed wording around the values (a test holds the ratio), which is what Meta's review looks at. Filled with the worst values that are allowed (the Malayalam wording, a 120-character name, a 60-character shop name, five-digit counts, the stand-in tag, a real 32-digit folder name) the longest message is about 830 characters, so there is about 190 of margin under the 1024 that WhatsApp checks once the values are in (a test holds 900); a shop name longer than about 250 characters would eat it, and nothing caps a shop name today. A test fills each template and compares it, letter for letter, with the plain text. They are informational only: no offer, no call to action; the badge line is a name, not a promotion. Meta decides the category in the end (Deferred.md).

The wording lives in `report/whatsapp.ts`, not in `src/language/meta-templates.ts`. Those templates are tied one to one to a message in `templates.ts` that is filled from a booking's facts, and three tests rest on that; the report is filled from a report. The script `whatsapp-templates.ts`, the stand-in and the tests join the two lists.

### The page: a link, not a PDF or an image

The page cannot be turned into a PDF or a PNG without a dependency. The report is as often in Hindi, Malayalam or Kannada as in English, and drawing those scripts needs a font engine that shapes them (letters reorder and join), or a headless browser. A PDF made with only the standard fonts would be English only. WhatsApp also does not take an HTML file as a document (it takes PDF, plain text, Word, Excel and PowerPoint). So the page goes as a link, which the owner's phone opens with its own fonts, in the owner's language, and which is the existing self-contained page (`report/html.ts`, no script, no external file).

The runtime writes the month's files with the same `writeClientReport` the builder's tools use, into `<dataDir>/reports/<32 random hex digits>/`: the page, the certificate (page and text), the owner text and the signed data. The folder name is the only secret in the address, which is why it is random and recorded in the entry (`refs.page`). The report is signed with the key `report.signingKeyFile` names (without one, the page says "Not signed" and the digest and code still identify it). The message carries the report's **check code**, and the page shows the same code and digest, so the owner's copy is checked against the message and against the builder's, and `scripts/verify-report.ts` checks `report-<month>.json` as it does any report (`geometer-verify` itself does not read these files yet, see Deferred.md). What the code does and does not show is on the page, as on every report.

Serving the folder is not done here (Deferred.md). That is why the job is off unless `runtime.json` has a `report` block: a template that starts sending must not start messaging owners with links that do not open.

`sendDocument` is added anyway, because a file by its address is how WhatsApp takes the certificate's text (plain text is an accepted document type). It is a session message, so it is refused outside the window, in the live adapter and in the fake, with the same error as free text (131047). The job uses it once, when the owner's window happens to be open, after the report text: the certificate's text under its file name, with the certificate's code as the caption. If WhatsApp refuses it nothing else changes. The live adapter sends `{type: "document", document: {link, filename, caption}}`; the stand-in accepts it, and the fake keeps `kind: "document"` and the file in the outbox for the tests.

### The job (`report/monthly-job.ts`)

- **When.** From 09:00 on the 1st, in the shop's time zone (`settings.timezone`), for the month that just ended. 09:00, not midnight: the owner is not woken, and a webhook that arrived late on the last night has been handled by then (the report counts an entry in the month it was written, so a later one belongs to the next month). If the process was down, the first tick after it is back sends it. A month before the last one is never made up.
- **Once, even across a crash.** The key is the shop (one record is one shop) and the month, `refs.report = "2026-11"`, on `send_message` entries to the owner. The runtime normally saves a step whole, after the step; for this job that would let a crash between "WhatsApp took it" and "saved" send it again. So the job saves twice: first an entry with outcome `attempted` (the claim), then the send, then a second entry with the ending (`confirmed`; `unconfirmed` when WhatsApp took it but could not show it back, or the reply was lost; `failed` when it was refused). A month with any entry `attempted`, `confirmed` or `unconfirmed` is never sent again, because each of them may have gone out. A claim with no ending (the process died in between) is closed with `unconfirmed` ("may or may not have reached the owner") at the next tick and not sent. Only `failed` is tried again, and `failed` means only a refusal that says nothing was sent: an answer with a 4xx status (a template that is not approved, a wrong number of values), the connection refused, or Guard not able to decide (`nothingWasSent`). A timeout, a lost reply, a 5xx (WhatsApp may have taken the message and failed after it), a reply with no message id, or an error nobody expected is `unconfirmed` and never sent again: `say()` already treats those as "may have gone out", and a retry here would be a second report to the owner. A retry is an hour later, three tries in all; then the builder is told in the log and the files are in the folder. The job rides on the tick, inside the runtime's one-step-at-a-time queue, after the agent's own work, and a report that cannot be made is logged once an hour and never holds up the tick (reminders and expiring holds still run).
- **A quiet month.** A month in which the record holds nothing but this job's own entries (or nothing) gets no message and no entry. A page of zeros tells the owner nothing they do not know, and the first message of the month should not be noise. The job looks again after a restart and finds the same.
- **In the record.** The entries are `send_message` to the owner (`refs.to = "owner"`), so the report counts them among the messages to the owner, and no new action name exists for the demo page or the cost meter to learn.
- **The cost meter.** `bundle/lib/usage.ts` `whatsappTypeOf` already counts a `send_message` to the owner that was `confirmed` or `unconfirmed` as utility, and an `attempted` one as nothing, which is exactly the pair this job writes. Only its comment changed; `bundle/test/usage.test.ts` feeds it the entries the real job wrote. Inside the window the text is a service message, so counting it as utility leans high, as for owner notices.

### Switching it on: the `report` block of `runtime.json`

```json
"report": { "preparedBy": "Divya, local partner", "pageBaseUrl": "https://reports.example.com/r", "innovator": false,
            "signingKeyFile": "keys/report-key.pem", "goLiveFile": "go-live-certificate.json", "source": "live", "pagesDir": "data/reports" }
```

`preparedBy` and `pageBaseUrl` are required; the rest is optional. `source` defaults to `stand-in` in fakes mode and `test-mode` in live mode (a report that says "test money" when it was real is a smaller mistake than the other way round). `preparedBy` and `innovator` are read again whenever `runtime.json` changes (like `ai`), so the bundle can write fleet's answer for the shop and the next report carries the badge, or not, with no restart; a half-written or unusable file leaves them as they were. A signing key that cannot be read stops the start with a plain sentence, rather than failing on the 1st.

### The badge (B56)

`innovator: true` goes into the report itself, so it is in the signed bytes; the page header, the certificate (page and text) and the message's badge line all read that one field. It is left out of the data when false, so every report made without it keeps its digest and code (a test holds that). The word is "Innovator" with a plain `★` (not an emoji, so every phone draws it alike), and a test checks that no badge string says "founding" or "founder". (Since 7 Nov the sell guides do not say "founder" either: a town is ready when "Geometer" says so.) `scripts/client-report.ts --innovator` shows it for a hand-made report.

## Recovery steps and the owner's agreement to a handover (B31, B38; added 7 November 2026)

The agent handles two more things, both small and both through the paths it already has. A **`recovery` event** (the panel's to-do list files a request, the deployer carries it to the runtime's `POST /internal/recover`) names one booking and an action: `recheck_payment` runs `reconcilePayments` (the payments service's own list for the booking's link, the one-deposit rule and all), `retry_booking` runs `completeBooking` for a booking the calendar would not confirm (the customer hears "booked" only if the calendar now shows it), `ask_owner_again` sends the owner the same notice again (`owner_cant_confirm`, or `owner_late_cancellation`), and `recheck_refund` looks, read-only, for a refund whose outcome was unclear and sends nothing. An action that does not fit, or a booking that is not there, is a `recovery` / `blocked` line and changes nothing. The record keeps a `recovery` entry for each (`refs.action`, `refs.recovery`). What the actions are and how the to-do list finds the bookings that need them is in `modules/Explanation.md`. **"move CODE"** from the owner's number writes one `handover_agreed` entry with the code's hash and nothing else (no reply, not an `owner_decision`, so the monthly report does not count it). Tests: `test/recovery.test.ts`.

## UPI, marked paid by the owner (B33; added 7 November 2026)

A shop picks how customers pay the deposit: `"payment": {"module": "upi", "upi": {"id": "glowsalon@okicici", "payee": "Glow Salon"}}` (the default the panel offers a
partner shop: no gateway account, no gateway fee) or `{"module": "razorpay"}`. A settings file with no `payment` is a Razorpay shop, and parses to the same object as
before, so nothing set up earlier changes. The shared module is `modules/payments/upi` (its Explanation section says why each part is as it is); this is what Bookings
does with it.

- **The quote.** The slot is held as always; the request is made through the same "look first, write, read back" (`ensureLink`), but the "link" is a `upi://pay` link
  to the shop's own UPI ID for exactly the deposit, with the booking number as its note. The customer gets the QR picture of that link (inside the window), then the
  words with the link, the UPI ID and the amount, and the buttons I have paid, Cancel booking and Talk to the owner. The owner gets "UPI payment to check", with the
  one-use code and a "Received ₹200" button (a WhatsApp template when the owner has not written today, which asks them to reply "received <code>").
- **Booked** only when the owner has marked the whole deposit received (`owner_marked_paid`), and the calendar shows it: `checkConfirmOnlyWhenRecorded` accepts the
  owner's marks adding up to the deposit, and nothing a customer sends. The customer's words say the owner confirmed it ("Booked! ... The owner has confirmed your ₹200
  deposit").
- **A different amount:** less is not booked; the hold stays; the owner is asked for the rest with a new code (the used one counts nothing again). If the hold runs out,
  the part is owed back by the owner. More books it, and the extra is owed back.
- **Never confirmed:** the hold ends at `holdMinutes` (the UPI example shop holds for 60, since the owner looks in their app); the request is closed in the record and the
  customer is told the owner had not confirmed it in time.
- **Twice:** one mark; the owner is told it was already counted.
- **After the hold:** recorded as received, never booked again by itself. The customer is told the owner will contact them; the owner gets Refund sent and Kept for
  booking (for a booking they make by hand); the builder sees a `late_upi` count in the status, and the money owed back is on the unfinished-work list.
- **Refunds** are owed by the owner (`upi_refund_owed`): inside the window at once, after it when the owner approves (`approve b-3`, as before; there is no Guard and no
  refund limit hold, because the owner sends every UPI refund). The customer is told the owner will send it, and "sent" only after the owner's mark.
- **"I paid", a screenshot, the I have paid button:** thanks, the owner will confirm, a screenshot is not proof; the owner is asked to look, at most once an hour.
- **Crash test:** on a UPI shop the situations written around a Razorpay link are named as not run, and nine UPI situations run (`crash-test/upi.ts`); Glow Salon (UPI)
  holds 9 of 9. The Razorpay shops are unchanged: Glow Salon 38 of 38, the equipment rental 33 of 33. The not-tested list of a UPI shop says that automatic payment
  checking does not apply to UPI and that no real UPI payment was made.
- **The report** labels a UPI shop's money "Deposits marked paid by owner" and "Refunds marked sent by owner" (the owner's lines, the page, the monthly certificate,
  and two more WhatsApp report templates, `monthly_report_upi` and `monthly_report_upi_innovator`). A Razorpay shop's report keeps its bytes.

## Syntax rules used

Only erasable TypeScript: no enums, namespaces, parameter properties or decorators. Type-only imports use `import type`. Imports carry their `.ts` extension. Files that read the settings JSON use `new URL(…, import.meta.url)`, so they work from any directory.
